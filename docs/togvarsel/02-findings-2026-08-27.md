# Togvarsel — findings (2026-08-27, morning)

*Can Entur tell Oslo commuters at 06:30 whether the trains will be trouble in the morning rush,
and again in the afternoon rush — like a weather forecast?*

**Short answer.** For the **morning rush: yes**, with useful skill today. For the **afternoon rush,
forecast in the morning: only weakly** — the ripple your hypothesis describes is real and
measurable, but it explains a minority of bad afternoons; an afternoon forecast becomes
credible around 12:00–14:00. Everything below is measured on 16.5 months of SIRI-ET/SX history
for Vy/NSB trains in the Oslo area (≈ 360 weekdays). Total BigQuery spend: ≈ $0.50.

Progress report 1 (`01-progress-2026-08-26.md`) documents the data build; this report supersedes
its numbers where they differ. SQL for every table, model and figure is in `sql/`.

**Update 2026-08-28.** §13 audits what kix-avvik already knew and the first rounds missed (the
product's trouble bar is ≥ 10 min; placebo arms; the per-call stack). §14 re-runs everything with
placebo arms and bootstrap CIs, on the 10-minute bar, with boosted trees and `disruptions.categories`
as features: the 06:30 morning forecast holds (AUC 0.74–0.86), **logistic regression beats the
trees**, categories add direction more than AUC, and the **07:00 issue for the rest of the rush is
the sharpest product** (green → 1 % bad). §14k lists what that changes in §8. Spend for the round
≈ $1.7, of which $1.64 is the BigQuery ML boosted trees (§14j); total for the exploration ≈ $2.3.

## 1. What "trouble" means here

Scope: Vy/NSB (`dataSource = 'VYG'`) rail calls at 39 stations between **Asker – Oslo S – Lillestrøm**
and **Oslo S – Ski**, incl. Nationaltheatret, Skøyen, Lysaker, Sandvika. Source:
`ent-data-sharing-ext-prd.realtime_siri_et.realtime_siri_et_last_recorded` (VYG rail exists there from
**2025-04-10**, so the history is 16.5 months, not two years).

`trouble` = share of calls in a time window that were **cancelled or ≥ 5 min late** (arrival, else departure).

| Weekday window (Oslo time) | median | p75 | p90 | p95 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| early 04:30–06:30 | 5 % | 9 % | 15 % | 21 % |
| **AM rush 06:30–09:00** | 13 % | 20 % | 30 % | 37 % |
| midday 09–15 | 9 % | 15 % | 21 % | 29 % |
| **PM rush 15–18** | 16 % | 26 % | 36 % | 43 % |
| evening 18–24 | 9 % | 13 % | 24 % | 32 % |

Labels: **AM bad** = AM trouble ≥ 25 % (17 % of weekdays); **PM bad** = PM trouble ≥ 30 % (15 %).
The afternoon is structurally worse than the morning: hourly trouble peaks at 20 % (08:00) and
22 % (16:00), and the cancellation rate climbs through the day (1.9 % at 06:00 → 3.5 % at 17:00).

## 2. The ripple is real — and it is system-wide

**Early morning → morning rush** (356 weekdays):

| Known by ~06:30 | days | P(AM bad) | P(PM bad) |
|---|---|---|---|
| base rate | 356 | 17 % | 15 % |
| early (04:30–06:30) trouble ≥ 10 % | 78 | **40 %** | 21 % |
| early trouble < 10 % | 278 | 10 % | 13 % |
| early cancellations ≥ 2 % of calls | 71 | 37 % | 20 % |
| previous evening (18–24) trouble ≥ 20 % | 37 | 30 % | 24 % |
| early bad **and** previous evening bad | 14 | **57 %** | **43 %** |

corr(early, AM) = 0.37. Morning trouble is strongly correlated **across corridors** — west ↔
northeast 0.72, west ↔ central 0.87, west ↔ south 0.58 — which is exactly what a shared bottleneck
(Oslotunnelen / Oslo S) predicts: a problem on one side of the city is felt on the other.

**By line**, the ripple is strongest on the high-frequency local lines with tight turnarounds:

| Line | corr(early, AM) | corr(early, PM) | corr(AM, PM) |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 Spikkestad–Lillestrøm | **0.45** | 0.21 | 0.28 |
| L2 Ski–Stabekk | 0.36 | −0.04 | 0.14 |
| R14 Asker–Kongsvinger | 0.33 | 0.08 | 0.19 |
| R13 Drammen–Dal | 0.31 | 0.00 | 0.08 |
| R21 Moss–Stabekk | 0.29 | −0.03 | 0.19 |
| R12 Kongsberg–Eidsvoll | 0.22 | 0.11 | 0.08 |
| RE11 Skien–Eidsvoll | 0.14 | 0.05 | 0.05 |

Corridor-level, the **northeast (Lillestrøm)** side has the strongest early→AM link (0.52, vs west
0.27, south 0.37) and is the only corridor where the early morning says anything about the
afternoon (0.29).

## 3. …but the ripple into the afternoon is weak

- AM bad → P(PM bad) = **32 %** vs 11 % after a normal morning (corr(AM, PM) = 0.23).
- Only **~37 %** of bad afternoons had a bad morning. Most bad afternoons are new events.
- How predictability of the **PM rush** builds through the day — correlation between the
  cumulative trouble-since-04:00 and PM-rush trouble, by the hour the forecast is issued:

| issued at | 05 | 06 | 07 | 08 | 09 | 10 | 11 | 12 | 13 | 14 | 15 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| corr with PM rush | 0.13 | 0.15 | 0.20 | 0.23 | 0.26 | 0.29 | 0.32 | 0.34 | 0.38 | 0.43 | 0.53 |
| P(PM bad \| cum. trouble ≥ 15 %) | 26 % | 26 % | 26 % | 21 % | 21 % | 21 % | 23 % | 25 % | 29 % | 29 % | 35 % |
| P(PM bad \| cum. trouble < 15 %) | 14 % | 13 % | 12 % | 12 % | 11 % | 11 % | 11 % | 11 % | 10 % | 10 % | 7 % |

(For the AM rush the same curve is 0.20 at 05:00 and 0.58 at 06:00 — the morning is knowable
early; the afternoon is not.)

## 4. What SIRI-SX adds

NSB situations are **per-journey** messages with no `affected_lines`; they name journeys
(`VYG:DatedServiceJourney:2127_LLS-ASR_26-08-19`) and stops, so they were joined to ET journeys to
learn line and corridor (≈ 90 % match). On an average weekday, **64 situations are already known by
06:30** (published from 18:00 the evening before): 22 "Færre vogner" (short train — often
*"vi mangler et togsett"*, published 00:00–04:00 for the whole day), 8.5 "innstilt", 17 morning-rush
journeys already flagged for something.

Univariate lift for **AM bad** (top quartile of the feature vs the rest):

| Signal known by 06:30 | P(AM bad) top | rest | r |
|---|---|---|---|
| ET trouble 04:00–06:20 | 33 % | 12 % | 0.32 |
| ≥ 2 journeys with a cancelled call before 06:20 | 42 % | 14 % | 0.31 |
| rush-hour journeys with an "innstilt" SX message | 31 % | 13 % | 0.28 |
| "innstilt" situations (any journey) | 26 % | 15 % | 0.23 |
| infrastructure / signal / power situations | 26 % | 15 % | 0.24 |
| previous evening trouble | 21 % | 17 % | 0.18 |
| "Færre vogner" / "mangler togsett" | 20 % | 17 % | −0.06 / 0.07 |
| planned reductions (buss for tog, stengt, færre avganger) | 17 % | 18 % | −0.04 |

Two surprises worth keeping in mind for the product:

1. **Missing train sets do not predict delays.** "Færre vogner / mangler et togsett" is exactly the
   "rolling stock out of position" signal the hypothesis names, and it is announced early — but it
   correlates with *nothing* in ET. Short trains are a **crowding** problem, which ET cannot see.
   A passenger forecast should still show it, as a known fact, not as a probability.
2. **Planned reductions correlate slightly negatively with delay-trouble.** When Vy has already
   announced "buss for tog" or "færre avganger", the trains that do run tend to run on time. Again:
   surface as *known deviations*, separate from the probabilistic forecast.

Weather (Frost stations in the Oslo box, Apr 2025 → Jun 2026) is a minor factor in this period:
snow on the ground raises P(AM bad) to 25 % (vs 15 %) but does nothing for the afternoon; cold,
rain and wind correlate ≤ 0.11 with either target. Weather is not the missing afternoon predictor.

## 5. Models

BigQuery ML logistic regression, evaluated on **two time-folds** that never see their test period
(A: train ≤ 2026-03, test 2026-04 → 2026-08, 98 weekdays; B: train ≥ 2025-10, test 2025-04 → 2025-09,
117 weekdays). Features: ET state at the cutoff, SX counts known by the cutoff, previous evening,
weekday, low-season flag.

| Forecast | AUC A | AUC B | corr(pred, actual trouble share) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **AM rush, issued 06:30** (15 features) | **0.76** | **0.73** | 0.44 / 0.43 |
| AM rush, issued 06:30 (6 features) | 0.72 | 0.72 | |
| PM rush, issued 06:30 | 0.57 | 0.59 | 0.21 / 0.08 |
| PM rush, issued 09:00 | 0.58 | 0.55 | 0.15 / 0.04 |
| PM rush, issued 12:00 | 0.63 | 0.54 | 0.37 / 0.02 |
| PM rush, issued 14:00 | 0.66 | 0.65 | 0.43 / 0.20 |

Strongest AM weights: ET trouble before 06:20 (+), cancellations before 06:20 (+), rush-hour journeys
already flagged in SX (+), previous evening (+); planned reductions (−); Fridays and July/holidays
are much calmer (−). PM models are unstable between folds — there is little to learn from the
morning; the 14:00 model is the first one that holds up on both folds.

**Calibration of the AM forecast** (out-of-fold, 215 days): predicted 0–5 % → 4 % observed;
5–10 % → 11 %; 10–20 % → 7 %; 20–30 % → 21 %; 30–50 % → 21 %; ≥ 50 % → 43 %. Usable; slightly
over-confident at the top.

## 7. The ceiling: sudden-onset events

Of 60 bad mornings, only **6** had a fully quiet early morning (no early trouble, no cancelled
journeys, no rush-hour SX flags, quiet previous evening) — so some signal existed on 90 % of bad
days. But the *worst* morning in the data (2026-03-16: 50 % of rush-hour calls cancelled) was one of
them: at 06:55 SX said *"Oslo S er stengt for togtrafikk. Dette skyldes en jordfeil."* No 06:30
forecast can see that. For the afternoon, 16 of 52 bad afternoons (31 %) followed a quiet morning
and midday. A forecast must say this out loud: "ingen kjente problemer" is not "ingen problemer".

## 8. Recommendation for the product

A "togmelding" issued at 06:30 with three parts:

1. **Morgenrushet** — green / yellow / red with the calibrated probability ("ca. 30 % sjanse for
   store forstyrrelser"), plus the *reason* (the features are all human-readable: "6 tog er allerede
   innstilt", "forsinkelser på Lillestrøm-siden siden kl 05", "problemer i går kveld").
2. **Kjente avvik i dag** — the SX facts already published: buss for tog, kortere tog (crowding),
   planned works. Deterministic, not probabilistic.
3. **Ettermiddagsrushet** — in the morning only two states: *"normalt utsikter"* or *"noe forhøyet
   risiko"* (after a bad morning: 32 % vs 11 %), with an explicit **update at 12:00 and 14:00**
   where the forecast becomes real (red at 14:00 → 39 % bad, 9 % of calls cancelled).

Skill to expect from day one: green mornings ≈ 2× safer than average, red mornings ≈ 2–3× worse;
44 % of days are green. That is roughly the skill of a 2-day rain forecast — a fair thing to call
a weather forecast.

## 9. Limitations and next steps

- **History is short** (16.5 months, 356 weekdays, 60 bad mornings). The AM result is stable across
  folds; PM numbers move with the fold. `kix-avvik`'s `disruptions.rail_calls` (production ET) could add
  Dec 2024 → Apr 2025.
- **`last_recorded` is a daily batch.** A live forecast needs the same features from
  `ent-anshar-prd.realtime.siri_realtime_et_production` + SX at 06:30; kix-avvik already computes
  per-call predictions from those feeds, so the plumbing exists.
- **Labels are mine.** ≥ 5 min / 25 % / 30 % thresholds were chosen from the distributions; a
  product should tie them to what passengers call a bad day (survey or app feedback).
- **Corridor-level forecasts** look worth building (northeast side is more predictable than west);
  a per-line forecast for L1/L2 is the natural next experiment.
- **Crowding is invisible** in ET; "Færre vogner" is the only proxy and it is strong at 06:30.
- **Unexplored, cheap to try:** Bane NOR planned-works feed as a feature; rolling-stock position
  via train numbers (the `2127_LLS-ASR` journey key encodes the train number — consecutive
  turnarounds could be chained); a text embedding of the 06:30 SX message set.

## 11. Follow-ups run 2026-08-27 morning

### 11a. Corridor-level forecasts beat the area-wide one
Same recipe (logistic regression, two time-folds), one row per corridor × day, target = corridor
AM trouble ≥ 25 %. Compared against the area-wide model's prediction evaluated on the *same* corridor labels.

| Corridor | fold | AUC corridor model | AUC area model | bad mornings when scored < 10 % (corridor / area) | bad mornings when scored ≥ 30 % (corridor / area) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| west (Asker–Oslo S) | A | **0.84** | 0.77 | **0 %** / 8 % | 47 % / 40 % |
| west | B | 0.78 | 0.81 | **0 %** / 2 % | 42 % / 44 % |
| northeast (Oslo S–Lillestrøm) | A | **0.82** | 0.80 | **0 %** / 8 % | 67 % / 54 % |
| northeast | B | 0.76 | 0.74 | 8 % / 12 % | 44 % / 48 % |
| south (Oslo S–Ski) | A | 0.68 | 0.67 | 0 % / 3 % | 9 % / 7 % |
| south | B | 0.68 | 0.61 | 11 % / 13 % | 30 % / 27 % |

The product should forecast **per corridor**: the green bucket becomes genuinely clean on the west and
northeast sides (0 % bad on 3 of 4 corridor-folds), and a red on the northeast side means a bad
morning roughly every second time. The south side (Ski) is the least forecastable in both models.
(Per-corridor test sets are small — 68–117 days, 7–20 bad mornings — so treat the exact numbers as indicative.)

### 11b. Afternoon cancellations are no more forecastable from the morning than lateness
Cancellation-based labels (share of rush-hour calls cancelled): corr(AM, PM) = 0.22. SX flags on
afternoon journeys known at 06:30 correlate 0.05 with afternoon cancellations (0.07 at 09:00,
0.26 at 12:00); "mangler togsett" 0.09; early ET cancellations 0.09. Days with ≥ 5 afternoon
journeys already flagged at 06:30 (39 days) had PM cancellations ≥ 5 % on 23 % of days vs 13 %.
Morning cancellations, by contrast, *are* visible early: rush-hour "innstilt" flags at 06:30
correlate 0.32 with the morning cancellation rate. Conclusion unchanged: the afternoon needs a midday update.

### 11c. The turnaround mechanism, measured directly
For every terminus turnaround (a train's last call → the next outbound departure of the same line
from that station within 45 min, as a proxy for the same train set):

| Line | turnarounds | planned turn | P(outbound ≥ 5 min late \| inbound ≥ 10 min late) | …\| inbound on time | P(outbound cancelled \| inbound ≥ 10 late) | …\| on time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | 49 121 | 16 min | **54 %** | 1.7 % | **8.9 %** | 0.6 % |
| L2 | 51 378 | 15 min | 32 % | 2.9 % | 7.5 % | 1.2 % |
| R13 | 31 689 | 16 min | 56 % | 1.4 % | 2.3 % | 0.2 % |
| R21 | 33 619 | 19 min | 41 % | 1.4 % | 4.6 % | 0.3 % |
| R14 | 16 385 | 18 min | 41 % | 1.9 % | 1.3 % | 0.2 % |
| RE11 | 18 318 | 11 min | 30 % | 1.5 % | 1.8 % | 0.1 % |
| RE20 | 15 311 | 26 min | 32 % | 2.2 % | 0.7 % | 0.1 % |
| R12 | 19 207 | 12 min | 11 % | 1.6 % | 0.7 % | 0.0 % |

A late inbound train turns into a late outbound one on roughly every second turnaround on L1/R13,
and into a **cancelled** outbound ~15× more often than normal on L1/L2. Since the pairing is a proxy
(the next departure is sometimes a different set), the true coupling is if anything stronger. This
is the "train sets out of position" effect, measured — and it is why the ripple is strongest on the
local lines and why cancellations cluster after an early disruption. (2.6 % of turnarounds start
≥ 10 min late on L1; on a bad morning it is a large multiple of that.)

## 12. Second round of follow-ups (2026-08-27)

### 12a. Line-level models (L1, L2) add nothing over corridor level
Same recipe, one row per line × day (features: the line's own early trouble/cancellations/mean
delay, its SX flags, plus the area-wide state), compared with the area model on the same line labels.

| Line | fold | AUC line model | AUC area model | bad when scored < 10 % (line / area) | days scored < 10 % (line / area) | bad when ≥ 30 % (line / area) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | A | 0.80 | 0.76 | **0 %** / 12 % | 3 / 25 | 50 % / 46 % |
| L1 | B | 0.82 | 0.83 | **0 %** / 4 % | 23 / 46 | 47 % / 44 % |
| L2 | A | 0.68 | 0.76 | 5 % / 2 % | 21 / 48 | 16 % / 13 % |
| L2 | B | 0.75 | 0.66 | 8 % / 13 % | 13 / 31 | 53 % / 36 % |

Differences of ± 0.05 are noise at 68–117 test days per line. The line model's green bucket is clean on
L1 but much smaller (it is less confident), so it buys precision with coverage. Strongest line-level
weights: the line's rush-hour journeys already flagged "innstilt" (+0.45), the line's early mean delay
(+0.33), early trouble (+0.24); planned reductions on the line (−0.28). **Corridor level is the right
granularity** with this much history; revisit line level after another year of data.

### 12b. Real train-set chaining is not available from the open feed
`ent-anshar-prd.realtime.siri_realtime_et_production` carries `vehicleRef` for 100 % of VYG journeys, but
it is the **train number** (2105, 2106, …, each used by two dated journeys a day), not a physical set;
`blockRef` is empty. So the turnaround pairing in §11c remains the best available proxy for "the
same set", and a true rolling-stock chain would need Vy's circulation plan (togsettomløp) as an
external input. Also checked: `occupancy` is empty for VYG in the production feed — crowding stays
invisible, and "Færre vogner" stays the only proxy. Cost note: pulling the scalar journey → vehicle
mapping for the full 16.5 months would have been 171 GB ≈ $1.07 (not run — the 1-day check settled it).

## 13. What kix-avvik already knew — context the first two rounds missed (2026-08-27, evening)

The exploration was run from the kix-labs checkout by accident, so it did not read kix-avvik's
`CLAUDE.md`, memory notes, `docs/*/LEARNINGS.md` or the PRDs. Audit of what that cost, ranked:

1. **The product's definition of "trouble" is ≥ 10 min, not ≥ 5.** Every kix-avvik surface uses
   *cancelled OR departure delay ≥ 10 min*: `varsel.TROUBLE_DELAY_MIN`, the nightly outcome labels
   (`data/queries/label_varsel_decisions.sql`), `category_line_hour_impact.trouble_rate`, and the alert
   composite `p_impact = p_cancel + (1 − p_cancel)·p_ge10`. Sections 1–12 are on trouble5 with
   25 %/30 % day thresholds. Section 14 restates the headline results on the 10-minute bar
   (`am_t10 ≥ 0.10` → 62 bad mornings, the same base rate as `am_t5 ≥ 0.25` → 60; the two shares
   correlate 0.90).
2. **Methodology rule paid for on 2026-08-19 (`congestion-features-dont-help`): always run a
   permutation-placebo arm, and distrust small eval windows.** Adding columns alone moved BQML
   boosted-tree AUC by ±0.009–0.016, and a 19-day window flipped the sign of an 80-day result. The
   comparisons in §5 and §11–12 (15 vs 6 features, corridor vs area, line vs corridor) are differences
   of that magnitude on folds with ~30 bad days — none had a placebo. Section 14 adds them.
3. **The per-call stack already encodes the ripple at its own grain.** `own_prev_delay`
   (attribution ≈ 0.45) and `line_now_*` (line state over the last 60 min, `predict_calls.sql`) are the
   live propagation features; `corridor_*` / `stop_now_*` were A/B'd negative even at Oslo S. The two
   questions differ — a day-level outlook hours ahead vs a per-train alert 30–60 min out (38 % of
   impacted departures alertable ≥ 30 min ahead, 5 % ≥ 2 h) — but the togvarsel forecast is the
   *complement* of that stack, and a live version should read `line_now_*` rather than build new
   plumbing.
4. **§9's "extend history via `rail_calls`" is smaller than claimed.** `rail_calls` starts
   2024-06-01 but has ~no usable Oslo journeys before the 2024-12-15 timetable change (12 vs 757
   journeys/day at Oslo S), and SX↔ET joins only work from ~May 2025. The gain is Dec 2024–Apr 2025 with
   ET-only features — one extra winter, no SX. `rail_calls` also recovers ~6 % more cancellations than
   `last_recorded`'s flags (3,808 vs 3,592 in May 2026).
5. **The SX cause taxonomy (`disruptions.categories`, AI.CLASSIFY, 16-way) was not used; §4 wrote a
   parallel regex `cause`.** kix-avvik's own result softens this — onset *status* (which §4 did use)
   out-predicts cause (η² 0.32 vs 0.23) — but the canonical, already-served label is `categories`
   (NULL-safe IFNULL join, 99.8 % coverage of the togvarsel situations). Its `drift/kapasitet` =
   benign-anchor finding matches the "Færre vogner is uncorrelated" result. Section 14 uses it.
6. **Weather is a *frequency* signal for the evening before, not a 06:30 signal.** Huskehjulet
   measured sporveksel failures 2.5× as frequent at tmin < −5 °C and materiell 2.2× with snow on the
   ground — "preparedness, not duration". At 06:30 that is already visible in ET (the "informative but
   redundant" shape kix-avvik keeps finding), which is why §4 saw only a minor effect. The experiment
   vault's **#11 Kollektivværmeldingen** (forecast at 22:00 the night before, alert only at > 70 %
   P(> 10 min)) is the variant where weather is the *only* input; §14 measures it.
7. **Product framing in `docs/kix-docs/prd/proaktivt-avvik/`.** P5 (pendlerassistent) is "stille som
   standard — 90+ % of days, nothing"; a three-part togmelding every morning pulls the other way, so §8
   must say whether it is a pull surface or pushes only on yellow/red. P4 (kaskade-copilot) is the
   ops-side twin (per-line P(delay > 5 min) at +10/20/30/60 min, `tunnel_utilization`,
   `rolling_stock_displacement` from SIRI-VM). Experiment #18 (Tillitsindikator) and team-data's
   `empirical_delay_v2` are the unconditional baseline the forecast should be stated against.
8. **Smaller:** holidays are a hard-coded 18-date list in `build_features.sql` (kix-avvik has none
   either — issue #16); a per-cycle CTAS for a forecast would feed the dev Data-Lineage cost line (use a
   query or a scheduled query); the "2026-08-10 idea triage" that memory links to is not in the repo.

What the exploration *did* pick up from kix-avvik: `sx_events` is built on kix-avvik's
`disruptions.disruptions` (rebuilt with its own `create_disruptions.sql`), using the leak-free
`onset_summary`/`onset_description` and the regex `status` column; weather came from kix-avvik's
Frost load; `realtime_siri_et_last_recorded` is the source kix-avvik's memory recommends for training.

## 14. Re-checks, boosted trees and categories (2026-08-27 → 28, overnight)

Everything in this section is on the same 340 eligible weekdays (2025-04-11 → 2026-08-25) and three
evaluation protocols: **A** (train < 2026-04-01, test 98 days), **B** (train ≥ 2025-10-01, test 117
days, both as in §5), and **ROLL** (rolling origin: retrain each month on everything before it, test
that month, Oct 2025 → Aug 2026 = 223 pooled out-of-sample days, 46 bad mornings). Every AUC carries a
95 % bootstrap interval over days; every "adding X helps" claim has a **placebo arm** (the extra
columns dealt to the wrong days, 20 permutations for LR / 10 for trees — the rule from
`congestion-features-dont-help`). Two model families: logistic regression (log1p on count columns,
standardized, L2 strength chosen by 5-fold CV inside the training fold) and gradient-boosted trees
(xgboost: depth 3, 200 trees, learning rate 0.05, subsample/colsample 0.8, min child weight 5, mean of
3 seeds). Sweeps ran locally on exported tables (`analysis/sweep.py`, `analysis/sweep_corr.py`); the
production-relevant configurations were re-trained in BigQuery ML for parity (§14j). New feature
table `disruptions.exp_togvarsel_features2` (`sql/build_features2.sql`, 164 columns): a 07:00 cutoff
with rest-of-rush labels, `disruptions.categories` counts per cutoff (22:00 the evening before,
06:30, 07:00, 12:00, 14:00), Frost weather, and evening-before SX state.

### 14a. On the product's bar (cancelled OR ≥ 10 min) the morning forecast holds

Labels: `am_t10 ≥ 0.10` (62 of 340 mornings, same base rate as `am_t5 ≥ 0.25` → 60; the two shares
correlate 0.90), `pm_t10 ≥ 0.15` (57), and a cancellation-day label `am_cancel ≥ 0.05` (45).
AUC [95 % CI], logistic regression unless stated:

(base rate 20 %), yellow 51 % → 21 %, **red 13 % → 55 % bad, 8 % of rush-hour calls cancelled**.

only 6–9 months of training behind them — see 14f.

Label sensitivity: the 06:30 model trained on trouble5 scores 0.72 against trouble10 and
**0.80–0.83 against the cancellation labels** — what it really detects is a *cancellation morning*.
Rank correlation with the continuous trouble share is only ~0.3.

### 14c. Boosted trees do not beat logistic regression here

Paired bootstrap of AUC(xgb) − AUC(LR) over 168 (target × features × protocol) cells: 15 intervals
exclude zero — **13 in favour of LR, 2 for trees** (one of those is the weather cell where LR broke on
the missing months). Fold A: LR 0.81–0.86 vs trees 0.67–0.75 on every AM feature set; fold B and
ROLL roughly tied. Trees are also worse calibrated: predicted vs observed bad-day rate per quintile is
2/4/8/14/40 vs 4/14/16/27/42 % for xgb against 7/10/12/17/36 vs 9/18/13/18/44 % for LR, so trees put
56 % of days in "green" at a 10 % bad rate where LR's green (32 % of days) is 11 % — the trees are
over-confident at the low end. With 250–340 training days and signal that is mostly monotone
(shares, counts), an additive model is the right size. A tuning grid over tree depth 1–4, learning
rate 0.02–0.05, 100–600 trees and stronger regularisation is in `analysis/xgb_tune.py`
(results in 14j).

### 14d. Categories from `disruptions.categories`

The AI.CLASSIFY taxonomy joins 99.8 % of the togvarsel situations (IFNULL-sentinel join on
`onset_summary`/`onset_description`). Per cutoff, the features are distinct situations per category
(13 groups after merging the dead leaves) and rush-hour journeys flagged by an infrastructure /
materiell / drift message. Their effect is small and never negative (14b). What they contribute is
*direction* rather than AUC — standardized LR coefficients on the full set: `et_t5_0630` +0.30,
**`cat_drift_personale` −0.19** (announced crew shortages mean planned, orderly reductions, not
chaos), `et_cx_0630` +0.18, `sx_big` −0.14 (announced buss-for-tog days are calmer than their
message count suggests), `prev_pm_t5` +0.13, `catj_infra_am` positive. Tree gain puts
**`et_t5_last_0630` — the state of 05:50–06:20 — first** in every AM configuration: the most recent
half hour matters most, which is the same fact as 14e.

### 14e. The 07:00 update is the sharpest product in the study

Forecasting the *remainder* of the rush (07:00–09:00, label `am_t5_rest ≥ 0.25`, 71 of 340 days)
from the 07:00 state gives **0.91 / 0.77 / 0.84** (A / B / ROLL) against 0.82 / 0.76 / 0.72 from the
06:30 state on the same label — placebo z of 4–9 (14b). Three levels at 07:00 (ROLL, LR with
categories): **green 32 % of days → 1 % bad**, yellow 51 % → 25 %, **red 17 % → 61 % bad, 7 % of
calls cancelled**. A passenger who leaves at 07:30 is better served by a 07:00 forecast for 07–09
than by a 06:30 forecast for 06:30–09:00.

### 14f. Skill is seasonal

ROLL AUC by quarter (trouble5, F15, LR): 2025Q4 0.65, 2026Q1 0.63, **2026Q2 0.75, 2026Q3 0.81**;
on the 10-min bar 0.72 / 0.70 / 0.80 / 0.78. The winter quarters have 24–25 % bad mornings against
9 % in spring 2026 and are harder to call at 06:30 (more sudden-onset infrastructure failures). It is
the season, not the training-set size: from 2026-01, with ≥ 9 months of history behind each retrain,
pooled ROLL AUC is 0.71 (trouble5) / 0.76 (trouble10), no better than the full window. Fold B
(tested Apr–Sep 2025) and fold A (Apr–Aug 2026) are both summer-half folds, which is why §5's
0.73–0.76 reads higher than a full-year rolling estimate. Monitoring should report skill by season.

### 14g. Evening-before forecast (vault experiment #11, "Kollektivværmeldingen")

Issued at 22:00 the evening before, from yesterday's outcome, the 5-day history, SX known by
22:00 (mostly announced works and route changes) and their categories: **0.77 / 0.57 / 0.64**.
Calendar + yesterday alone gives 0.77 / 0.53 / 0.60 — the evening forecast is mostly "yesterday was
bad", and fold B says that does not transfer. Adding the *actual* next-day weather (an upper bound on
any forecast) changes nothing (0.77 / 0.58 / 0.51). Bins: red 6 % of days → 50 % bad, green 25 % →
11 % (base 20 %). Verdict: not a probabilistic product on its own; what the evening can carry is
the *deterministic* list of announced deviations for tomorrow.

### 14h. PM, again

PM from the 06:30 state is at chance on every label (0.50–0.58); at 14:00, 0.63–0.73 (trouble5),
0.61–0.70 (trouble10); PM **cancellation** days at 14:00 are the most forecastable PM target
(xgb 0.78 / 0.66 / 0.71, LR 0.75 / 0.59 / 0.69). Categories add nothing to any PM cutoff. Unchanged
from §3 and §11b.

### 14j. BigQuery ML parity (LOGISTIC_REG vs BOOSTED_TREE_CLASSIFIER) and the tuning grid

**Tuning grid** (`analysis/xgb_tune.py`, 8 tree configurations × fixed-C logistic regression, mean of
5 seeds). Strong regularisation helps the trees a lot in the folds — depth 2–3 with min child weight
10 reaches 0.78 / 0.79 (A / B) on the 10-min bar with categories, and 0.79 / 0.80 with the extra ET
state — but the same configurations fall to 0.68 / 0.72 in ROLL, where the looser trees sit at
0.71–0.75. Logistic regression with C = 0.1 is the best single configuration on every AM target:
trouble10 + categories **0.84 / 0.75 / 0.74**, trouble10 + extra **0.84 / 0.71 / 0.78**, rest-of-rush
at 07:00 **0.89 / 0.77 / 0.83**; the best tree matches or beats it on one protocol (fold B) and loses
on the other two. On cancellation days the two families tie (0.80 / 0.70–0.78 / 0.74–0.77). Verdict
unchanged: trees are not better here, and the tuned ones that come closest are the ones that behave
most like an additive model.

**BQML parity.** Across the 12 (configuration × fold) cells BQML `LOGISTIC_REG` averages AUC 0.697 against 0.686 for `BOOSTED_TREE_CLASSIFIER` (same hyper-parameters as the local trees), and the logistic model wins 6 of 12. Wall-clock: 41 s per logistic model vs 9 min per boosted tree — BQML bills a boosted tree per iteration, so 200 iterations on 250 rows bill ~19 GB each: the 14 boosted-tree jobs (12 + 2 reruns) billed 263 GB ≈ $1.64 and 38 slot-hours, the 12 logistic ones 0.3 GB ≈ $0.002 (`sql/bqml_cost.sql`, from `INFORMATION_SCHEMA.JOBS_BY_PROJECT`). In BigQuery ML as trained here — raw counts, a fixed `l2_reg=0.5`, no log1p — the two families are an honest tie: the trees win fold B, the logistic model wins fold A. That plain BQML logistic model also trails the locally tuned LR by 0.03–0.09 in fold A (0.76 vs 0.81, 0.78 vs 0.82, 0.74 vs 0.83) and matches it in fold B, so the local edge came from the count transform and the CV-chosen regularisation, not from the algorithm. The production path is therefore `LOGISTIC_REG` **with log1p on the count columns and `l2_reg` tuned on a held-out month** (the local optimum, C = 0.1, is a strong penalty), on the 15 + extra-ET-state columns, retrained monthly — a 40-second job, better calibrated than the tree (green at 6–8 % bad vs 6–12 %, red at 33–56 % vs 33–45 %), and explainable.

### 14k. What this changes in the recommendation (§8)

1. **Build on the product's bar.** Trouble = cancelled OR ≥ 10 min; the 06:30 forecast keeps its
   skill there (0.74–0.86) and its green/red separation (6 % vs 55 % bad).
2. **Logistic regression, not trees** — but *tuned*: log1p on count columns and a strong, held-out-tuned
   `l2_reg`. Untuned BQML `LOGISTIC_REG` only ties the boosted tree (§14j); tuned, it is better on the
   folds, better calibrated, explainable, and trains in 40 s instead of 7–20 min.
3. **Feature set = F15 + extra ET state (+ categories for the reason text).** No weather, no rolling
   persistence, no kitchen sink.
4. **Two issues, not one:** 06:30 for the whole rush, **07:00 for the rest of it** (green → 1 % bad).
   The 07:00 issue is the one to show a commuter who leaves at 07:30.
5. **Corridor level: keep the corridor-specific early state, skip cross-corridor features.**
6. **Evening-before: deterministic only** (announced deviations), no probability.
7. **Report skill by season**; winter mornings are a harder forecast, and the product copy should
   say so ("vinterhalvåret er vanskeligere å varsle").
8. **Every future claim carries a placebo arm and a CI** — half of the deltas in §5/§11/§12 were
   inside placebo churn.
