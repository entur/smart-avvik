# Togvarsel — findings (2026-08-27, morning)

*Can Entur tell Oslo commuters at 06:30 whether the trains will be trouble in the morning rush,
and again in the afternoon rush — like a weather forecast?*

**Short answer.** For the **morning rush: yes**, with useful skill today. For the **afternoon rush,
forecast in the morning: only weakly** — the ripple your hypothesis describes is real and
measurable, but it explains a minority of bad afternoons; an afternoon forecast becomes
credible around 12:00–14:00. Everything below is measured on 16.5 months of SIRI-ET/SX history
for Vy/NSB trains in the Oslo area (≈ 360 weekdays). Total BigQuery spend: ≈ $0.50.

Progress report 1 (`01-progress-2026-08-26.md`) documents the data build; this report supersedes
its numbers where they differ. SQL for every table, model and figure is in `sql/`.

---

## 1. What "trouble" means here

Scope: Vy/NSB (`dataSource = 'VYG'`) rail calls at 39 stations between **Asker – Oslo S – Lillestrøm**
and **Oslo S – Ski**, incl. Nationaltheatret, Skøyen, Lysaker, Sandvika. Source:
`ent-data-sharing-ext-prd.realtime_siri_et.realtime_siri_et_last_recorded` (VYG rail exists there from
**2025-04-10**, so the history is 16.5 months, not two years).

`trouble` = share of calls in a time window that were **cancelled or ≥ 5 min late** (arrival, else departure).

| Weekday window (Oslo time) | median | p75 | p90 | p95 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| early 04:30–06:30 | 5 % | 9 % | 15 % | 21 % |
| **AM rush 06:30–09:00** | 13 % | 20 % | 30 % | 37 % |
| midday 09–15 | 9 % | 15 % | 21 % | 29 % |
| **PM rush 15–18** | 16 % | 26 % | 36 % | 43 % |
| evening 18–24 | 9 % | 13 % | 24 % | 32 % |

Labels: **AM bad** = AM trouble ≥ 25 % (17 % of weekdays); **PM bad** = PM trouble ≥ 30 % (15 %).
The afternoon is structurally worse than the morning: hourly trouble peaks at 20 % (08:00) and
22 % (16:00), and the cancellation rate climbs through the day (1.9 % at 06:00 → 3.5 % at 17:00).

## 2. The ripple is real — and it is system-wide

**Early morning → morning rush** (356 weekdays):

| Known by ~06:30 | days | P(AM bad) | P(PM bad) |
|---|---|---|---|
| base rate | 356 | 17 % | 15 % |
| early (04:30–06:30) trouble ≥ 10 % | 78 | **40 %** | 21 % |
| early trouble < 10 % | 278 | 10 % | 13 % |
| early cancellations ≥ 2 % of calls | 71 | 37 % | 20 % |
| previous evening (18–24) trouble ≥ 20 % | 37 | 30 % | 24 % |
| early bad **and** previous evening bad | 14 | **57 %** | **43 %** |

corr(early, AM) = 0.37. Morning trouble is strongly correlated **across corridors** — west ↔
northeast 0.72, west ↔ central 0.87, west ↔ south 0.58 — which is exactly what a shared bottleneck
(Oslotunnelen / Oslo S) predicts: a problem on one side of the city is felt on the other.

**By line**, the ripple is strongest on the high-frequency local lines with tight turnarounds:

| Line | corr(early, AM) | corr(early, PM) | corr(AM, PM) |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 Spikkestad–Lillestrøm | **0.45** | 0.21 | 0.28 |
| L2 Ski–Stabekk | 0.36 | −0.04 | 0.14 |
| R14 Asker–Kongsvinger | 0.33 | 0.08 | 0.19 |
| R13 Drammen–Dal | 0.31 | 0.00 | 0.08 |
| R21 Moss–Stabekk | 0.29 | −0.03 | 0.19 |
| R12 Kongsberg–Eidsvoll | 0.22 | 0.11 | 0.08 |
| RE11 Skien–Eidsvoll | 0.14 | 0.05 | 0.05 |

Corridor-level, the **northeast (Lillestrøm)** side has the strongest early→AM link (0.52, vs west
0.27, south 0.37) and is the only corridor where the early morning says anything about the
afternoon (0.29).

## 3. …but the ripple into the afternoon is weak

- AM bad → P(PM bad) = **32 %** vs 11 % after a normal morning (corr(AM, PM) = 0.23).
- Only **~37 %** of bad afternoons had a bad morning. Most bad afternoons are new events.
- How predictability of the **PM rush** builds through the day — correlation between the
  cumulative trouble-since-04:00 and PM-rush trouble, by the hour the forecast is issued:

| issued at | 05 | 06 | 07 | 08 | 09 | 10 | 11 | 12 | 13 | 14 | 15 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| corr with PM rush | 0.13 | 0.15 | 0.20 | 0.23 | 0.26 | 0.29 | 0.32 | 0.34 | 0.38 | 0.43 | 0.53 |
| P(PM bad \| cum. trouble ≥ 15 %) | 26 % | 26 % | 26 % | 21 % | 21 % | 21 % | 23 % | 25 % | 29 % | 29 % | 35 % |
| P(PM bad \| cum. trouble < 15 %) | 14 % | 13 % | 12 % | 12 % | 11 % | 11 % | 11 % | 11 % | 10 % | 10 % | 7 % |

(For the AM rush the same curve is 0.20 at 05:00 and 0.58 at 06:00 — the morning is knowable
early; the afternoon is not.)

## 4. What SIRI-SX adds

NSB situations are **per-journey** messages with no `affected_lines`; they name journeys
(`VYG:DatedServiceJourney:2127_LLS-ASR_26-08-19`) and stops, so they were joined to ET journeys to
learn line and corridor (≈ 90 % match). On an average weekday, **64 situations are already known by
06:30** (published from 18:00 the evening before): 22 "Færre vogner" (short train — often
*"vi mangler et togsett"*, published 00:00–04:00 for the whole day), 8.5 "innstilt", 17 morning-rush
journeys already flagged for something.

Univariate lift for **AM bad** (top quartile of the feature vs the rest):

| Signal known by 06:30 | P(AM bad) top | rest | r |
|---|---|---|---|
| ET trouble 04:00–06:20 | 33 % | 12 % | 0.32 |
| ≥ 2 journeys with a cancelled call before 06:20 | 42 % | 14 % | 0.31 |
| rush-hour journeys with an "innstilt" SX message | 31 % | 13 % | 0.28 |
| "innstilt" situations (any journey) | 26 % | 15 % | 0.23 |
| infrastructure / signal / power situations | 26 % | 15 % | 0.24 |
| previous evening trouble | 21 % | 17 % | 0.18 |
| "Færre vogner" / "mangler togsett" | 20 % | 17 % | −0.06 / 0.07 |
| planned reductions (buss for tog, stengt, færre avganger) | 17 % | 18 % | −0.04 |

Two surprises worth keeping in mind for the product:

1. **Missing train sets do not predict delays.** "Færre vogner / mangler et togsett" is exactly the
   "rolling stock out of position" signal the hypothesis names, and it is announced early — but it
   correlates with *nothing* in ET. Short trains are a **crowding** problem, which ET cannot see.
   A passenger forecast should still show it, as a known fact, not as a probability.
2. **Planned reductions correlate slightly negatively with delay-trouble.** When Vy has already
   announced "buss for tog" or "færre avganger", the trains that do run tend to run on time. Again:
   surface as *known deviations*, separate from the probabilistic forecast.

Weather (Frost stations in the Oslo box, Apr 2025 → Jun 2026) is a minor factor in this period:
snow on the ground raises P(AM bad) to 25 % (vs 15 %) but does nothing for the afternoon; cold,
rain and wind correlate ≤ 0.11 with either target. Weather is not the missing afternoon predictor.

## 5. Models

BigQuery ML logistic regression, evaluated on **two time-folds** that never see their test period
(A: train ≤ 2026-03, test 2026-04 → 2026-08, 98 weekdays; B: train ≥ 2025-10, test 2025-04 → 2025-09,
117 weekdays). Features: ET state at the cutoff, SX counts known by the cutoff, previous evening,
weekday, low-season flag.

| Forecast | AUC A | AUC B | corr(pred, actual trouble share) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **AM rush, issued 06:30** (15 features) | **0.76** | **0.73** | 0.44 / 0.43 |
| AM rush, issued 06:30 (6 features) | 0.72 | 0.72 | |
| PM rush, issued 06:30 | 0.57 | 0.59 | 0.21 / 0.08 |
| PM rush, issued 09:00 | 0.58 | 0.55 | 0.15 / 0.04 |
| PM rush, issued 12:00 | 0.63 | 0.54 | 0.37 / 0.02 |
| PM rush, issued 14:00 | 0.66 | 0.65 | 0.43 / 0.20 |

Strongest AM weights: ET trouble before 06:20 (+), cancellations before 06:20 (+), rush-hour journeys
already flagged in SX (+), previous evening (+); planned reductions (−); Fridays and July/holidays
are much calmer (−). PM models are unstable between folds — there is little to learn from the
morning; the 14:00 model is the first one that holds up on both folds.

**Calibration of the AM forecast** (out-of-fold, 215 days): predicted 0–5 % → 4 % observed;
5–10 % → 11 %; 10–20 % → 7 %; 20–30 % → 21 %; 30–50 % → 21 %; ≥ 50 % → 43 %. Usable; slightly
over-confident at the top.

## 7. The ceiling: sudden-onset events

Of 60 bad mornings, only **6** had a fully quiet early morning (no early trouble, no cancelled
journeys, no rush-hour SX flags, quiet previous evening) — so some signal existed on 90 % of bad
days. But the *worst* morning in the data (2026-03-16: 50 % of rush-hour calls cancelled) was one of
them: at 06:55 SX said *"Oslo S er stengt for togtrafikk. Dette skyldes en jordfeil."* No 06:30
forecast can see that. For the afternoon, 16 of 52 bad afternoons (31 %) followed a quiet morning
and midday. A forecast must say this out loud: "ingen kjente problemer" is not "ingen problemer".

## 8. Recommendation for the product

A "togmelding" issued at 06:30 with three parts:

1. **Morgenrushet** — green / yellow / red with the calibrated probability ("ca. 30 % sjanse for
   store forstyrrelser"), plus the *reason* (the features are all human-readable: "6 tog er allerede
   innstilt", "forsinkelser på Lillestrøm-siden siden kl 05", "problemer i går kveld").
2. **Kjente avvik i dag** — the SX facts already published: buss for tog, kortere tog (crowding),
   planned works. Deterministic, not probabilistic.
3. **Ettermiddagsrushet** — in the morning only two states: *"normalt utsikter"* or *"noe forhøyet
   risiko"* (after a bad morning: 32 % vs 11 %), with an explicit **update at 12:00 and 14:00**
   where the forecast becomes real (red at 14:00 → 39 % bad, 9 % of calls cancelled).

Skill to expect from day one: green mornings ≈ 2× safer than average, red mornings ≈ 2–3× worse;
44 % of days are green. That is roughly the skill of a 2-day rain forecast — a fair thing to call
a weather forecast.

## 9. Limitations and next steps

- **History is short** (16.5 months, 356 weekdays, 60 bad mornings). The AM result is stable across
  folds; PM numbers move with the fold. `kix-avvik`'s `disruptions.rail_calls` (production ET) could add
  Dec 2024 → Apr 2025.
- **`last_recorded` is a daily batch.** A live forecast needs the same features from
  `ent-anshar-prd.realtime.siri_realtime_et_production` + SX at 06:30; kix-avvik already computes
  per-call predictions from those feeds, so the plumbing exists.
- **Labels are mine.** ≥ 5 min / 25 % / 30 % thresholds were chosen from the distributions; a
  product should tie them to what passengers call a bad day (survey or app feedback).
- **Corridor-level forecasts** look worth building (northeast side is more predictable than west);
  a per-line forecast for L1/L2 is the natural next experiment.
- **Crowding is invisible** in ET; "Færre vogner" is the only proxy and it is strong at 06:30.
- **Unexplored, cheap to try:** Bane NOR planned-works feed as a feature; rolling-stock position
  via train numbers (the `2127_LLS-ASR` journey key encodes the train number — consecutive
  turnarounds could be chained); a text embedding of the 06:30 SX message set.

## 11. Follow-ups run 2026-08-27 morning

### 11a. Corridor-level forecasts beat the area-wide one
Same recipe (logistic regression, two time-folds), one row per corridor × day, target = corridor
AM trouble ≥ 25 %. Compared against the area-wide model's prediction evaluated on the *same* corridor labels.

| Corridor | fold | AUC corridor model | AUC area model | bad mornings when scored < 10 % (corridor / area) | bad mornings when scored ≥ 30 % (corridor / area) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| west (Asker–Oslo S) | A | **0.84** | 0.77 | **0 %** / 8 % | 47 % / 40 % |
| west | B | 0.78 | 0.81 | **0 %** / 2 % | 42 % / 44 % |
| northeast (Oslo S–Lillestrøm) | A | **0.82** | 0.80 | **0 %** / 8 % | 67 % / 54 % |
| northeast | B | 0.76 | 0.74 | 8 % / 12 % | 44 % / 48 % |
| south (Oslo S–Ski) | A | 0.68 | 0.67 | 0 % / 3 % | 9 % / 7 % |
| south | B | 0.68 | 0.61 | 11 % / 13 % | 30 % / 27 % |

The product should forecast **per corridor**: the green bucket becomes genuinely clean on the west and
northeast sides (0 % bad on 3 of 4 corridor-folds), and a red on the northeast side means a bad
morning roughly every second time. The south side (Ski) is the least forecastable in both models.
(Per-corridor test sets are small — 68–117 days, 7–20 bad mornings — so treat the exact numbers as indicative.)

### 11b. Afternoon cancellations are no more forecastable from the morning than lateness
Cancellation-based labels (share of rush-hour calls cancelled): corr(AM, PM) = 0.22. SX flags on
afternoon journeys known at 06:30 correlate 0.05 with afternoon cancellations (0.07 at 09:00,
0.26 at 12:00); "mangler togsett" 0.09; early ET cancellations 0.09. Days with ≥ 5 afternoon
journeys already flagged at 06:30 (39 days) had PM cancellations ≥ 5 % on 23 % of days vs 13 %.
Morning cancellations, by contrast, *are* visible early: rush-hour "innstilt" flags at 06:30
correlate 0.32 with the morning cancellation rate. Conclusion unchanged: the afternoon needs a midday update.

### 11c. The turnaround mechanism, measured directly
For every terminus turnaround (a train's last call → the next outbound departure of the same line
from that station within 45 min, as a proxy for the same train set):

| Line | turnarounds | planned turn | P(outbound ≥ 5 min late \| inbound ≥ 10 min late) | …\| inbound on time | P(outbound cancelled \| inbound ≥ 10 late) | …\| on time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | 49 121 | 16 min | **54 %** | 1.7 % | **8.9 %** | 0.6 % |
| L2 | 51 378 | 15 min | 32 % | 2.9 % | 7.5 % | 1.2 % |
| R13 | 31 689 | 16 min | 56 % | 1.4 % | 2.3 % | 0.2 % |
| R21 | 33 619 | 19 min | 41 % | 1.4 % | 4.6 % | 0.3 % |
| R14 | 16 385 | 18 min | 41 % | 1.9 % | 1.3 % | 0.2 % |
| RE11 | 18 318 | 11 min | 30 % | 1.5 % | 1.8 % | 0.1 % |
| RE20 | 15 311 | 26 min | 32 % | 2.2 % | 0.7 % | 0.1 % |
| R12 | 19 207 | 12 min | 11 % | 1.6 % | 0.7 % | 0.0 % |

A late inbound train turns into a late outbound one on roughly every second turnaround on L1/R13,
and into a **cancelled** outbound ~15× more often than normal on L1/L2. Since the pairing is a proxy
(the next departure is sometimes a different set), the true coupling is if anything stronger. This
is the "train sets out of position" effect, measured — and it is why the ripple is strongest on the
local lines and why cancellations cluster after an early disruption. (2.6 % of turnarounds start
≥ 10 min late on L1; on a bad morning it is a large multiple of that.)

## 12. Second round of follow-ups (2026-08-27)

### 12a. Line-level models (L1, L2) add nothing over corridor level
Same recipe, one row per line × day (features: the line's own early trouble/cancellations/mean
delay, its SX flags, plus the area-wide state), compared with the area model on the same line labels.

| Line | fold | AUC line model | AUC area model | bad when scored < 10 % (line / area) | days scored < 10 % (line / area) | bad when ≥ 30 % (line / area) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | A | 0.80 | 0.76 | **0 %** / 12 % | 3 / 25 | 50 % / 46 % |
| L1 | B | 0.82 | 0.83 | **0 %** / 4 % | 23 / 46 | 47 % / 44 % |
| L2 | A | 0.68 | 0.76 | 5 % / 2 % | 21 / 48 | 16 % / 13 % |
| L2 | B | 0.75 | 0.66 | 8 % / 13 % | 13 / 31 | 53 % / 36 % |

Differences of ± 0.05 are noise at 68–117 test days per line. The line model's green bucket is clean on
L1 but much smaller (it is less confident), so it buys precision with coverage. Strongest line-level
weights: the line's rush-hour journeys already flagged "innstilt" (+0.45), the line's early mean delay
(+0.33), early trouble (+0.24); planned reductions on the line (−0.28). **Corridor level is the right
granularity** with this much history; revisit line level after another year of data.

### 12b. Real train-set chaining is not available from the open feed
`ent-anshar-prd.realtime.siri_realtime_et_production` carries `vehicleRef` for 100 % of VYG journeys, but
it is the **train number** (2105, 2106, …, each used by two dated journeys a day), not a physical set;
`blockRef` is empty. So the turnaround pairing in §11c remains the best available proxy for "the
same set", and a true rolling-stock chain would need Vy's circulation plan (togsettomløp) as an
external input. Also checked: `occupancy` is empty for VYG in the production feed — crowding stays
invisible, and "Færre vogner" stays the only proxy. Cost note: pulling the scalar journey → vehicle
mapping for the full 16.5 months would have been 171 GB ≈ $1.07 (not run — the 1-day check settled it).
