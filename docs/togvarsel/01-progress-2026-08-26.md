# Togvarsel — progress report 1 (2026-08-26, night)

**Question:** Can we forecast, early in the morning, whether Vy/NSB commuters in the Oslo area
will have trouble in the morning rush and the afternoon rush — like a weather forecast?
**Hypothesis under test:** early-morning disruptions ripple into the rush hours (and the whole
day) because track capacity is saturated and train sets end up out of position.

## Data set up (all in `ent-kixavvik-dev.togvarsel`, cost so far ≈ $0.25)

16.5 months (≈ 360 weekdays), not two years. (2) NSB situations carry **no `affected_lines`** —
they reference journeys (`VYG:DatedServiceJourney:2127_LLS-ASR_26-08-19`) and stops, so SX had to be
joined to ET journeys to know which line/corridor a message is about (≈ 90 % match).

## Definition of "trouble"

`trouble5` = share of Oslo-area calls in a window that were **cancelled or ≥ 5 min late**.
Weekday distribution:

| Window (Oslo local) | median | p75 | p90 | p95 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| early 04:30–06:30 | 4.8 % | 9 % | 15 % | 21 % |
| **AM rush 06:30–09:00** | 13 % | 20 % | 30 % | 37 % |
| midday 09–15 | 9 % | 15 % | 21 % | 29 % |
| **PM rush 15–18** | 16 % | 26 % | 36 % | 43 % |
| evening 18–24 | 9 % | 13 % | 24 % | 32 % |

Working labels: **AM bad** = AM `trouble5` ≥ 25 % (17 % of weekdays), **PM bad** = PM `trouble5` ≥ 30 %
(15 % of weekdays). The afternoon rush is structurally worse than the morning (hourly trouble
peaks at 20 % at 08:00 and 22 % at 16:00; cancellation rate climbs through the day from 1.9 % at
06:00 to 3.5 % at 17:00).

## Findings so far

### 1. Early-morning trouble does ripple into the morning rush — strongly
356 weekdays, Oslo area as a whole:

| Condition known at ~06:30 | n days | P(AM bad) | P(PM bad) |
|---|---|---|---|
| base rate | 356 | 17 % | 15 % |
| early (04:30–06:30) trouble ≥ 10 % | 78 | **40 %** | 21 % |
| early trouble < 10 % | 278 | 10 % | 13 % |
| early cancellations ≥ 2 % of calls | 71 | 37 % | 20 % |
| previous evening trouble ≥ 20 % | 37 | 30 % | 24 % |
| early bad **and** previous evening bad | 14 | 57 % | 43 % |

corr(early trouble, AM trouble) = 0.37. The effect is **system-wide**: AM trouble is highly
correlated across corridors (west↔northeast 0.72, west↔central 0.87, west↔south 0.58), which is
what a shared bottleneck (Oslotunnelen / Oslo S) predicts.

### 2. The ripple into the afternoon is real but weak
- AM bad → P(PM bad) = 32 % vs 11 % when the morning was fine. corr(AM, PM) = 0.23.
- But only ~37 % of bad afternoons had a bad morning; most bad afternoons are new events.
- Lead-time curve (corr of cumulative trouble since 04:00 with PM-rush trouble): 0.15 at 06:00,
  0.26 at 09:00, 0.34 at 12:00, 0.43 at 14:00, 0.53 at 15:00. Predictability of the afternoon
  builds slowly through the day; the morning alone tells us little.

### 3. SIRI-SX adds specific, early signals — some expected ones do not
NSB messages are per-journey. Known by 06:30 on an average weekday: 64 situations, 22 of them
"Færre vogner" (short trains, often "vi mangler et togsett" — published 00:00–04:00 for the
whole day), 8.5 "innstilt", 17 morning-rush journeys already flagged.

Univariate lift for **AM bad** (top quartile vs rest): ET trouble before 06:20 → 33 % vs 12 %;
≥ 2 journeys with cancelled calls before 06:20 → 42 % vs 14 %; rush-hour journeys with an
"innstilt" message by 06:30 → 31 % vs 13 %; infrastructure/signal/power situations → 26 % vs 15 %.

Surprises: "Færre vogner"/"mangler togsett" counts have **no** correlation with delay-trouble
(r ≈ −0.06 / 0.07) — short trains are a capacity problem, not a punctuality one, and ET cannot
see crowding. Planned reductions ("buss for tog", "stengt", "færre avganger") correlate slightly
*negatively* with delay-trouble: the trains that do run, run on time. For a passenger forecast
these should be surfaced as **known** deviations, separate from the probabilistic part.

### 4. First models (BigQuery ML logistic regression, two time-folds)
Test folds: A = 2026-04-01→2026-08-25 (98 weekdays), B = 2025-04-10→2025-09-30 (117 weekdays).

| Forecast | AUC fold A | AUC fold B | Days scored < 10 % | Observed bad-rate there |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **AM rush, issued 06:30** | **0.76** | **0.73** | ~45 % of days | 6.5–8 % (base 12–15 %) |
| PM rush, issued 06:30 | 0.57 | 0.59 | | |
| PM rush, issued 09:00 | 0.58 | 0.55 | | |
| PM rush, issued 12:00 | 0.63 | 0.54 | | |
| PM rush, issued 14:00 | 0.66 | 0.65 | | |

At p ≥ 0.30 the AM model flags ~15–20 % of days with precision ≈ 33 % (2–3× base rate) and recall
33–57 %. Strongest weights: ET trouble before 06:20, cancellations before 06:20, rush-hour journeys
already flagged in SX, previous evening trouble; Fridays and July/holiday periods are much calmer.

### 5. A hard ceiling: sudden-onset events
The worst morning in the data (2026-03-16, 50 % of rush-hour calls cancelled) had a clean early
morning; at 06:55 SX said "Oslo S er stengt for togtrafikk. Dette skyldes en jordfeil". No 06:30
forecast can see that. Several of the 20 worst mornings look like this.

## Preliminary answer
- **Morning rush:** yes — a "train weather forecast" at 06:30 is feasible with useful skill:
  a green/yellow/red with calibrated probabilities where green days really are ~2× safer than
  average and red days ~2–3× worse.
- **Afternoon rush, forecast in the morning:** the ripple exists but is too weak on its own for
  a confident forecast; honest output is "slightly elevated risk" after a bad morning, with a
  real afternoon forecast only becoming possible around 12:00–14:00.
