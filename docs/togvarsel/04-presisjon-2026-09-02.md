# Rushvarsel — why /rushhistorikk's Presisjon reads 40 %, and what would move it (2026-09-02)

*prd's /rushhistorikk showed Presisjon 40 % after three days (2 of 5 pushes justified). Is that low
against the history the models were trained on, and can the model be improved?*

**Short answer.** 40 % is not low — it is slightly *above* what the current models plus the
current push policy deliver on every window we can measure, in-sample or out-of-sample. Replayed
over the training data (Apr 2025 – Aug 2026, 343 weekdays) with the live models, the page's
Presisjon is **25 % over the whole period and 28–36 % over the last 14/30/90 days**; rolling
out-of-sample it is **25 %** (production recipe re-fitted month by month in BigQuery ML; 29 % in
the local sklearn replay). The
figure is capped by the *policy*, not the fit: a yellow push means p in [0.10, 0.30), the yellow
band is calibrated at ~15–23 % bad days, and yellow is ~70 % of all pushes (273 of 398 out-of-sample) — so by the page's own
definition (a yellow or red push is justified only when the rush turns yellow or red) roughly four
yellow pushes in five *must* read «Falsk alarm» however good the model is. The lever is the cut,
and moving it trades precision for bad-rush coverage along a curve the tables below make explicit.
Pushing only at red (p ≥ 0.30, a yellow *or* red rush counting as correct) lifts Presisjon to
**47–50 %** at roughly a quarter of the push volume, warning about 43 % of the bad rushes instead
of 81–91 %. On the model side: none of 13 candidate features survives a placebo arm, boosted trees
still lose, the AM models have real skill (BigQuery ML rolling OOS AUC 0.77 / 0.76), the 12:00
model has little (0.58), a stronger `l2_reg` buys nothing once calibration is counted, and the one
candidate worth a follow-up is a minimal feature set for the 14:00 model (+0.06 AUC, CI just
including zero). BigQuery spend for this note: under $1 (§7).

> **Decision (2026-09-02, evening).** The product owner changed the push rule to **red-only**
> (`togvarsel_notify.PUSH_LEVELS`), citing notifications perceived as noisy. As implemented a push
> says red or says green, never yellow: baselines push only at red (a yellow baseline is recorded
> as `yellow_baseline`), a red follow-up with nothing standing is a fresh warning, a green follow-up
> after a pushed red is the friskmelding, and a yellow follow-up is silent either way (`no_change`)
> — a red, if one stands, stands. Replayed exactly on the BigQuery ML rolling re-fit (226 OOS
> weekdays): **Presisjon 38 % (39/104), 0.46 pushes a day, 43 % of bad rushes warned**, against
> 25 % / 2.0 / 91 % under the launch rule; 34 % in the winter half, 42 % in the summer half. That
> is lower than §3a's 47 % because that row counted every drop below p 0.30 as a friskmelding,
> while the implemented rule retracts only at green — which the 07:00 and 14:00 models never
> reached after a red in the replay — and a yellow downgrade push was left out on purpose: the page
> grades a push on the level it says, and 21 of 23 such rushes turned green, so it would have read
> as a false alarm nine times in ten while adding a push on ~10 % of days. On the prd days the new
> rule would have sent one push (31 Aug 06:30, red, rush yellow: justified) instead of five.
> `analysis/rushsim.py` carries both rules (`push_levels`).

Everything below reuses the study's protocols (`02-findings-2026-08-27.md` §14: rolling-origin
monthly retrain Oct 2025 → Aug 2026, 226 OOS days; placebo arms; paired bootstrap CIs) and the
page's own verdict rules (`disruptions/api/rushhistorikk_verdicts.py`), replayed by
`analysis/rushsim.py` — fed prd's three days it reproduces the page's figures exactly (2/5, 2/4,
and every slot row). SQL and scripts are listed at the end.

## 1. What Presisjon measures, and what that implies

Presisjon = of the pushes that went out on scored issues, the share that were *justified*: a
yellow or red push on a rush that turned yellow or red (`trouble10 ≥ bar`, i.e. the training label
is TRUE), or a friskmelding on a rush that turned green. Dekning = of the situations where a push
was owed, the share that got a justified one — and a *yellow push on a green day creates a debt*
(a friskmelding is now owed at the follow-up), so low-confidence pushes depress Dekning too.

The push policy (`togvarsel_notify.decide_issue`) pushes a baseline (06:30, 12:00) whenever the
level is not green, i.e. whenever p ≥ 0.10. So the expected precision of the pushes is the mean
calibrated probability of the pushed forecasts. On the three prd days that is
(0.317 + 0.112 + 0.226 + 0.210 + (1 − 0.077)) / 5 = **36 %**; realised 2/5 = 40 %.

Observed bad-day rate per forecast level, live models (in-sample) and the production recipe
re-fitted rolling out-of-sample in BigQuery ML (§5):

| issue | green: days → bad | yellow: days → bad | red: days → bad |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0630 in-sample | 152 → 3 % | 127 → 15 % | 64 → 59 % |
| 0630 OOS (BQML) | 97 → 7 % | 86 → 23 % | 43 → 42 % |
| 0700 in-sample | 94 → 2 % | 179 → 16 % | 70 → 59 % |
| 0700 OOS (BQML) | 54 → 6 % | 120 → 19 % | 52 → 50 % |
| 1200 in-sample | 58 → 3 % | 260 → 17 % | 25 → 52 % |
| 1200 OOS (BQML) | 45 → 11 % | 158 → 18 % | 23 → 22 % |
| 1400 in-sample | 59 → 3 % | 242 → 14 % | 42 → 50 % |
| 1400 OOS (BQML) | 33 → 9 % | 164 → 15 % | 29 → 41 % |

In-sample the probabilities are almost perfectly calibrated (yellow p̄ 0.17 vs 15 % bad), which
is why "the days were in the training set" does not lift Presisjon: the model was never claiming
more than ~17 % for a yellow day. Out-of-sample the AM models are over-confident at the red end
(0630 red p̄ 0.61 → 42 % bad) and slightly under-confident at the green end; the 12:00 model's three
bands sit at 11 / 18 / 22 %, barely apart — see §4d and §5.

it to drift down toward ~30 % as the pushes accumulate, with 95 % binomial noise of ± 20
percentage points at n = 20 and ± 9 at n = 100.

## 3. The lever is the cut — the trade-off, out-of-sample

Same 226 OOS days (local sklearn replay; the BigQuery ML figures for the same cuts are in §3a),
policy replayed with alternative level cuts. "Bad rushes warned" is the traveller-centric recall the
page does not show: of the rushes that turned bad, the share that had
a yellow or red push at either of its two issues.

| policy (green < / red ≥) | Presisjon | Dekning | bad rushes warned | pushes / day |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **production 0.10 / 0.30** | **29 %** | 37 % | **81 %** | 1.76 |
| yellow from 0.15 | 34 % | 47 % | 68 % | 1.35 |
| yellow from 0.20 | 41 % | 53 % | 55 % | 1.00 |
| yellow from 0.25 | 45 % | 49 % | 45 % | 0.73 |
| red only (≥ 0.30) | 51 % | 45 % | 42 % | 0.57 |
| AM as production, 12:00 red only, 14:00 from 0.20 | 32 % | 39 % | 62 % | 1.08 |
| AM from 0.20, 12:00 red only, 14:00 from 0.20 | 41 % | 46 % | 55 % | 0.78 |
| 06:30 red only, 07:00 as production, PM as production | 28 % | 41 % | 84 % | 1.66 |

Three readings. (1) Precision and coverage are a straight trade along the models' ROC curve —
no cut gives both. Raising the yellow cut to 0.20 buys +12 points of Presisjon and halves the
push volume at the cost of a quarter of the bad rushes losing their warning. (2) Dekning, as the
page defines it, *rises* with a stricter cut because fewer wrong warnings mean fewer friskmelding
debts — that is the definition, not a product gain, and "bad rushes warned" is the honest recall.
(3) Letting 07:00 carry the morning (06:30 red only, 07:00 unchanged) keeps recall at 84 % and
costs nothing, which is §14e's finding again: the 07:00 issue is the sharp one.

### 3a. Red only, with a yellow or red rush counting as correct

Asked directly (2026-09-02 evening): push only when p ≥ 0.30, send a friskmelding if a later issue
drops below it, and count a push as justified when the rush turns yellow **or** red. The second
half is already the page's rule — a red push on a yellow rush is «traff nesten» and counts — so the
change is only the cut. BigQuery ML production recipe, rolling OOS, 226 weekdays:

| policy | Presisjon, all pushes | warnings only | bad rushes warned | pushes / day | expected Presisjon (mean p) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| production (push at p ≥ 0.10) | 25 % (114/455) | 22 % | 91 % | 2.0 | 28 % |
| yellow from 0.20 | 35 % (87/251) | 29 % | 62 % | 1.1 | 42 % |
| **red only (p ≥ 0.30)** | **47 %** (60/127) | 38 % (39/104) | 43 % | 0.56 | 56 % |
| red only, morning issues only | 49 % (39/80) | 41 % | 30 % | 0.35 | 62 % |
| push at p ≥ 0.40 | 57 % (47/83) | 50 % | 35 % | 0.37 | 65 % |
| push at p ≥ 0.50 | 60 % (30/50) | 57 % | 26 % | 0.22 | 72 % |

Per issue at red only: 06:30 42 %, 07:00 57 %, 12:00 22 %, 14:00 67 %. The local sklearn replay
gives the same shape (50 % / 40 % warnings-only / 41 % warned), in-sample 54 %. It is stable across
the seasons — 47 % in both Oct–Mar and Apr–Aug, where production reads 23 % and 27 % — because the
cut removes the yellow band whose bad rate is what moves with the season. "All pushes" runs above
"warnings only" because friskmeldinger are nearly always right (21 of 23).

Two limits. **Precision saturates in the 50s**: out-of-sample the red band is 40–50 % bad although
the model quotes 0.56–0.72 (the expected-Presisjon column), so the models are over-confident at the
top and raising the cut past 0.40 buys little precision while recall falls fast. Recalibration
would fix the quoted number, not the outcomes. And **the 12:00 issue stays weak at any cut** — 22 %
at red only, 5 of 23 red forecasts bad.

Whichever cut is chosen, the page should print the **expected Presisjon** (mean p of the pushes in
the window) beside the realised one. It is the number that says whether the model is doing what it
promised — 36 % expected against 40 % realised on the prd days — and without it a calibrated
forecast pushing at yellow reads as a failing product.

permutations of the added columns), z = net / sd(placebo). Candidate features are built by
`sql/build_features3.sql` from the chain's own tables.

Recency adds nothing because `et_t5_last_*` already carries it (§14d: the last half hour is the

§14b. The PM additions move the 14:00 model by +0.02–0.03 with CIs that include zero; a minimal
8-feature 14:00 set scores 0.651–0.664 (+0.04–0.06, CI [−0.03, +0.13]) — suggestive, tested in
BigQuery ML in §5.

### 4b. Model family: trees lose again

HistGradientBoosting (depth 2, 150 trees, strong regularisation) on the production lists: 0630
0.704 (−0.02), 0700 0.682 (−0.07, CI excludes 0), 1200 0.607 (+0.06, CI [−0.05, +0.19]), 1400
0.616 (+0.01). §14c stands.

### 4c. Regularisation: a local gain that BigQuery ML does not confirm

Sweeping the L2 strength on the production lists (sklearn C; BigQuery's `l2_reg` maps onto a
stronger region of this scale — see §5):

| model | production-mapped C | best C | OOS AUC gain | paired 95 % CI |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0630 | 0.333 → 0.725 | 0.03–0.05 → 0.768 | **+0.042** | [+0.008, +0.079] |
| 0700 | 0.100 → 0.750 | 0.02 → 0.764 | +0.015 | [−0.010, +0.044] |
| 1200 | 0.333 → 0.544 | any | ≤ +0.02 | includes 0 |
| 1400 | 0.100 → 0.607 | 0.01–0.02 → 0.639 | +0.03 | [−0.014, +0.080] |

In the sklearn replay stronger penalties help or do no harm everywhere and the 0630 gain is real
— but the BigQuery re-fit (§5) shows the production `l2_reg` already sits at that optimum: ten
times the penalty moves AUC by +0.01 to +0.04 with every interval including zero, and it **breaks
calibration** — at `l2_reg = 100` the 07:00 model puts zero days below 0.10 and 153 of 226 at or
above 0.30 (38 % bad, quoted 0.47), so under the fixed level cuts it would push red on two days
in three. The sklearn C-scale simply maps onto a weaker region than BigQuery's. What survives is the
protocol point: `10_train_models.sql` picks `l2_reg` by log-loss on the last 56 days (40 weekdays),
a criterion noisy enough that the same grid can land a decade apart between retrains, while a
rolling-origin log-loss over the last 8–11 months (44 LOGISTIC_REG jobs, cents) is the right size
for a monthly retrain and, being log-loss, keeps calibration in the objective.

### 4d. Skill is seasonal, and the 12:00 issue has little

Production recipe, OOS AUC by half-year (BigQuery ML, §5): 0630 **0.73** (Oct–Mar) / **0.83**
(Apr–Aug); 0700 0.72 / 0.84; 1200 0.53 / 0.65; 1400 0.59 / 0.71. Winter mornings have 24–29 % bad
days against 15–16 % in the summer half and are harder to call, as §14f found. The 12:00 model is at
chance in winter and weak in summer: its yellow pushes (0.7 per day, ~39 % of all pushes, 18–19 %
precision) are the single largest source of «Falsk alarm» on the page and carry little a traveller
can act on. Two options, either
of which the policy tables support: push the 12:00 issue only at red (22–30 % precision, about a
fifth of bad afternoons still caught early), or keep the 12:00 probability off the push channel altogether and
let 14:00 carry the afternoon (the study's §8 originally had the morning issue say only "normale
utsikter / noe forhøyet risiko" about the afternoon).

### 4e. Not tried, and why

- **More history** (`disruptions.rail_calls` reaches back to Dec 2024): the SX-journey features
  cannot be built before ~May 2025 (SX `affected_journeys` only join to ET refs from then —
  `sx-et-journey-ref-boundary`), so the extra winter would enter with half its features missing.
- **Corridor-level forecasts** (+0.02–0.04 in §14b): a product change, not a model change.
- **Recalibration** (Platt/isotonic on rolling OOS): would fix the 0630 red band's over-confidence
  (p̄ 0.61 → 42 % bad) but not the yellow band, which is where Presisjon is decided.
- **AutoML / DNN**: 340 rows; the additive model is the right size (§14c).

Readings, in order of weight:

- **The production recipe's honest skill** is 0630 0.765, 0700 0.757, 1200 0.580, 1400 0.651 —
  the AM figures at the study's §14a level, the PM ones below the tuning table's 0.75 (a 40-day
  summer holdout). By half-year: 0630 0.73 / 0.83, 0700 0.72 / 0.84, 1200 0.53 / 0.65, 1400

- **Stronger `l2_reg` is not an improvement** (§4c): the AUC moves are inside noise and the log-loss
  and level calibration get worse — the level cuts are on the probability, so a model that no longer
  puts any day under 0.10 has no green.
- **The minimal PM sets are the one lead**: 14:00 +0.063 with the interval just touching zero, and
  better log-loss and calibration than the baseline; 12:00 +0.045 with a wide interval. They add two
  features the production query does not compute — the morning rush's realised ≥ 10 share
  (`am_t10_known`, the ripple §3 of the study measured) and the 12:00–13:50 window — and drop the
  cumulative-since-04:00 shares. Before promoting: a placebo arm (the local one for the additions
  alone was z ≤ 0.8, but the minimal set also *removes* seven columns), and a serving-query twin.
- **Page rates on the BigQuery baseline, production policy**: Presisjon 25 % (114/455), Dekning
  31 %, 91 % of bad rushes warned, 2.0 pushes a day; trailing 14 / 30 / 90 / 180 days 35 / 38 /
  31 / 26 %. Red-only and the other cuts are in §3a. A combination of the AUC-best variants (l2 × 10
  for AM, minimal for PM) reads *worse* under the production policy — 22 %, 2.6 pushes a day — which
  is the calibration point made a third time.

## 6. Recommendations

1. **Read 40 % as on-target, not as low.** Print expected Presisjon (mean p of pushes) beside the
   realised figure on /rushhistorikk; on the prd days it is 36 %.
2. **Decide the cut as a product question**, with §3/§3a's tables (BigQuery, OOS): production
   (25 % / 91 % of bad rushes warned / 2.0 pushes a day), yellow from 0.20 (35 % / 62 % / 1.1), red
   only (47 % / 43 % / 0.56), p ≥ 0.40 (57 % / 35 % / 0.37). The cheapest single change is **12:00
   red-only**: −0.6 pushes a day and the morning untouched, though that slot stays weak at any cut.
3. **Keep `l2_reg` where it is, but switch the tuning criterion** to rolling-origin log-loss at the
   next monthly retrain (`10_train_models.sql`) — not for AUC, which does not move, but because the
   40-day holdout is noisy enough to land on a penalty that destroys calibration (§4c, §5).
4. **Leave the AM feature sets alone** (thirteen candidates, none clears placebo); **follow up the
   minimal 14:00 set** with a placebo arm and a serving twin before considering it (§5).
5. **Report skill by season** on the page (§14k point 7) — winter Presisjon will read lower with
   nothing about the model having changed.
