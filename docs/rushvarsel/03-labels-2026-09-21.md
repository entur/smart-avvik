# Retraining on corrected labels (2026-09-21)

Follow-up to [02-removed-journeys](02-removed-journeys-2026-09-21.md), which found that trains
Vy pulls without a `Cancellation` leave the rush labels reading too low on exactly the bad days.
This note asks the goal's question of that finding: does correcting the labels and retraining
move the accuracy of the four network forecasts? The area models of
[01-omraader](01-omraader-2026-09-21.md) are not re-run here; the label fix applies to them the
same way and nothing in the result below suggests it would change their verdict.

Cost of this note plus the removed-journeys investigation: 581 jobs, 96 `CREATE MODEL`
(logistic regressions only), 243 GB billed, **$1.52**. Boosted trees were not touched.

## The three label sets

All three are trouble10 shares over the 39 Oslo-area stations, same windows and bars as
production (`am_t10 ≥ 0.10`, `am_t10_rest0700 ≥ 0.10`, `pm_t10 ≥ 0.15`), rebuilt from
`togvarsel_calls` and checked to reproduce the chain's own labels exactly (358 of 358 eligible
days, all three windows).

| set | rule | what it fixes |
|---|---|---|
| **old** | resolved = cancelled or has a time; trouble = cancelled or ≥ 10 min | production today |
| **f2** | an `estimated = TRUE` time is not an observation; an estimated call whose journey went silent more than 10 min before the call was due is cancelled | the phantom tails of a journey pulled mid-run (shape B, departed half) |
| **f3** | f2, plus the planned Oslo-area calls of every GTFS train that never appeared in ET that day, on a line that did run, counted as cancelled | the undeparted half of shape B and any train the plan had that ET never carried |

The GTFS join is by train number (`ServiceJourney:2240_…` ↔ `DatedServiceJourney:2240_…`),
which matches 99 % or better on every Vy Oslo line but RE20 (16 % unmatched by numbering, so
excluded). The baseline noise is a median of zero unmatched trains a day and a p95 of one to
three; the signal days are unmistakable (2026-09-11: 30 PM trips, 2025-11-05: 28, 2026-08-12: 15
plus 16 in the morning). Both sets are in `disruptions.exp_rush_labels_fix` (dev), the GTFS
calls in `disruptions.exp_rush_gtfs_calls`.

**How many labels move.** f2 alone flips three PM days and nothing else: the phantom-tail
defect is real but small at the label. f3 flips 4 AM, 3 + 1 rest-of-rush and 7 PM days of 358
eligible, every one of them from green to bad. Two of them are the days the removed-journeys
note is about: 2026-09-11 PM goes 0.085 → 0.424 and 2025-11-05 PM goes 0.002 → 0.461.

## Results (rolling origin, 12 monthly folds Oct 2025–Sep 2026, 241 scored days)

`base` is today's recipe trained on the old labels (the same predictions as in 01-omraader);
`F2`/`F3` are the same recipe trained on f2/f3. Every model is scored against every label set.
Full table in `analysis/results/eval_labels.txt`.

| issue | base on old | base on f3 | F3 on f3 | F3 − base on f3 [95 % CI] |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 06:30 | 0.773 | 0.775 | 0.773 | −0.001 [−0.026, +0.023] |
| 07:00 | 0.768 | 0.753 | 0.755 | +0.001 [−0.013, +0.019] |
| 12:00 | 0.573 | 0.596 | 0.607 | +0.011 [−0.020, +0.042] |
| 14:00 | 0.635 | 0.638 | 0.658 | **+0.020 [−0.005, +0.045]** |

Three readings.

- **The label correction itself re-grades the PM forecast upward**: today's 12:00 model scores
  0.573 on the old labels and 0.596 on f3, because the old labels punished it for warning on
  days when the trains it warned about were quietly taken away. That is the same effect
  /rushhistorikk shows on 11 September (two yellows graded as unjustified).
- **Retraining on the corrected labels moves the PM models a little and the AM models not at
  all.** +0.011 at 12:00 and +0.020 at 14:00, intervals including zero; log-loss improves
  0.003–0.007. The AM slots have too few flipped days (4 of 241) to learn anything new.
- **f2 alone is neutral to slightly negative** (−0.014 at 12:00, significant), so the silent-tail
  rule is not worth adopting without the GTFS rule.

Level bins on f3 labels barely move (06:30 green 7 % bad either way; 14:00 red 41 % either way),
so the cuts stand.

## What this means

1. **The label bias is real and it lands on the scoreboard, not the model.** Adopting the f3
   rule in `03_day_windows.sql` and in both `togvarsel_outcomes` passes changes what the
   models are graded against: 15 green days become bad days, /rushhistorikk stops calling the
   11 September yellows wrong, and the retrained PM models pick up one to two AUC points that are
   not statistically secure. It is the right thing to do for the truth of the page, and a small
   thing for accuracy.
2. **It does not change 01-omraader's conclusion.** The area split remains a relevance gain;
   the pooled model P is still the shape to build; the south is still weak.
3. **The forecast itself is not the bottleneck on a removal day.** On 11 September the feature
   side saw a normal afternoon (the removal came at 15:04, after the 14:00 cutoff) and no SX
   said anything. No relabelling gives the 14:00 model a signal that did not exist at 14:00. The
   live signal that does exist is the GTFS-versus-ET count at issue time (today at 06:30: 46 L1
   departures fewer than the standing plan), which is a feature for a later retrain once the
   label side carries it — point 4 of 02-removed-journeys.

## What happened next

The owner decided the same evening that a timetable cut announced the day before counts as a bad
rush. [04-cuts](04-cuts-2026-09-21.md) builds that into the label (f4b) and into a feature and is
where the accuracy actually moved.
