# Rushvarsel — rush areas: does a per-direction forecast beat the area-wide one? (2026-09-21)

*Rushvarsel issues one forecast for "the Oslo area" (39 stations, Asker – Oslo S – Lillestrøm and
Oslo S – Ski). Would three or four forecasts — one per direction out of Oslo S — be more accurate
for the traveller who reads them, and can the models be improved on the way?*

**Short answer.** The split is worth making as a *relevance* change, not as an accuracy change.
Today's single forecast, judged by what happened in one direction, scores 0.70 (northeast), 0.73
(west), 0.63 (south) AUC at 06:30 rather than the 0.77 it scores on its own label — the area-wide
label disagrees with a direction's own on 9–12 % of mornings (§2). No per-area model recovers that
gap: models trained per direction lose 0.02–0.13 AUC to variance on ~300 days and collapse in the
south; the area's own state clears a placebo arm in exactly one cell. What does work is **one pooled
model per issue with area dummies** (P): the same AUC as today in every direction, better-calibrated
probabilities (log-loss 0.469 vs 0.517), and **+0.08 AUC in the northeast at 07:00**. The south is
close to unforecastable at this grain with any model (§4b, §7). `/rushhistorikk`'s Presisjon would not
move (20–26 % everywhere; it is the push-on-yellow policy). BigQuery spend: ≈ $3 at the analysis rate, but ≈ $132 if
the 17 boosted-tree models are billed at BigQuery ML's advanced rate — the tree wave was stopped for
that reason and is the one part of this note that may have exceeded the budget (§8).

**Product boundary.** The rush forecast is served by `/togmelding-api` (`disruptions/api/togvarsel.py`,
page `/togmelding`, pushes through `/varsel`) and is what this note measures. `/notification-api/v1`
carries the per-train alerts of the webhook channel and the journey-keyed pull read; it has no rush
forecast on it today. If the rush forecast is to be exposed there, the per-area question below is the
one that decides what shape it takes (one payload per area).

**What was already known** (`docs/togvarsel/02-findings-2026-08-27.md` §11a, §12a, §14b;
`04-presisjon-2026-09-02.md` §4a): corridor-specific models scored on corridor labels beat the
area-wide model on the same labels by **+0.02–0.04 AUC** with a placebo arm (§14b: "real, not §11a's
+0.07"), line-level added nothing over corridor level, cross-corridor features added nothing, and
per-corridor early state as an *extra feature on the area-wide model* added nothing (04-presisjon
§4a, +0.003). None of that was measured against the question a traveller asks — *how well does the
forecast I am shown describe MY rush* — nor on the production recipe (tuned `l2_reg`, log1p, category
block, the 07:00 rest-of-rush issue and the two PM issues), nor with the last year of data. That is
what this note does.

## 1. The areas

The four station groups of `disruptions.togvarsel_oslo_stations` (00_oslo_stations.sql), which are
already the directions out of Oslo S:

| area | stations | AM-rush calls / weekday (median) | share of AM calls | lines carrying them |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **west** | Skøyen … Asker (12) | 451 | 36 % | L1, L2, R12, R13, R14, R21, RE10, RE11, RX11, F4 |
| **northeast** | Bryn … Lillestrøm (12) | 277 | 22 % | L1 (87 %), R12, R13, R14, RE10, RE11 |
| **south** | Nordstrand … Ski (13) | 293 | 24 % | L2 (88 %), R21, R22, R23, RE20 |
| central | Oslo S, Nationaltheatret (2) | 217 | 18 % | every line |

Two schemes are tested: **3 areas** (the three directions; central excluded) and **4 areas**
(central as its own area). Central is where every line meets, so a traveller there has no single
direction; its label correlates 0.84 with the west's and is closer to a rollup than an area.

**Eligibility has to be per area.** The chain's day filter (`am_n > 300 AND pm_n > 300`) is tuned to
the 39-station set. Northeast has ~30 AM calls on 60 otherwise-eligible days and south ~29 on 45 —
the track-closure weeks (Oslo S – Lillestrøm and the Østfold line, April, July, October) when the
corridor is bus-served and its "rush" is a handful of regional trains. A per-area floor of **half the
area's median rush call count** (west 226 / 264, northeast 139 / 164, south 147 / 177, central
108 / 125 AM / PM) drops those days: of the 358 area-eligible weekdays (2025-04-10 → 2026-09-21),
west keeps 351, south 312, northeast 297, central 358. Nearly all of the loss is July 2025 (7 / 15 / 23
days west / northeast / south) and July 2026 (0 / 23 / 20), plus northeast in April and October 2025.
The bars stay the product's (AM 0.10, PM 0.15): trouble10 is a share per call, so it does not scale
with area size — but it gets noisier, which is one of the things the results have to absorb.

## 2. How often the area-wide label is wrong for one direction

Before any model: on the days both are defined, how often does the area-wide label (`am_t10 ≥ 0.10`
over all 39 stations) disagree with the area's own?

| area | days | area-wide bad | own bad | agree | area said bad, own fine | own bad, area said fine | corr(t10) | 07–09 agree | PM agree |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| west | 351 | 64 | 65 | **91 %** | 15 | 16 | 0.92 | 90 % | 95 % |
| northeast | 297 | 53 | 63 | **88 %** | 13 | 23 | 0.79 | **84 %** | 91 % |
| south | 312 | 58 | 60 | **88 %** | 18 | 20 | 0.69 | 82 % | 86 % |
| central | 358 | 64 | 53 | 92 % | 19 | 8 | 0.92 | 93 % | 92 % |

On realised *levels* (green / yellow / red at the bar and twice the bar) agreement is 82–89 % in the
morning; the area-wide level and the corridor's own are ever red-vs-green on 1–5 days per corridor.
The **rest-of-rush label (07–09, the 07:00 issue's) agrees least**: 84 % in the northeast and 82 % in
the south, against 88 % for the whole morning — the second half of the rush is where the directions
diverge most, which is where a per-direction label has most to gain (§4b).
Between corridors the picture is sharper: **northeast and south correlate only 0.28** on the AM trouble
share (0.56 PM), west–northeast 0.67, west–south 0.47 — the two outer directions are largely
independent mornings, held together in the area-wide label by the west and central stations that
carry 55 % of the calls.

So the ceiling for the split is about **12 % of mornings on the northeast and south sides** where a
perfect area-wide forecast tells the traveller the wrong class, and ~9 % on the west. A per-area
forecast can only beat the area-wide one on those days; on the other 88–91 % the two labels agree and
the question is whether the smaller area's noisier label costs skill.

## 3. Protocol

Rolling-origin, monthly retrain, **Oct 2025 → Sep 2026 (12 folds, 241 out-of-sample weekdays area-wide)**, the
production recipe in BigQuery ML (`LOGISTIC_REG`, log1p `TRANSFORM` on count columns, the tuned
`l2_reg` 3 / 10 / 3 / 10 per issue), the four issues (06:30 whole rush, 07:00 rest of rush, 12:00 and
14:00 for the PM rush). Data refreshed the same day through 2026-09-21 (`retrain.sh`'s 00–09 chain,
models NOT retrained or promoted). Feature table `disruptions.exp_rush_area_training`
(`sql/build_area_training.sql`): every area-wide column of `togvarsel_training` plus the area's own
state with suffix `_c` — the same ET cutoff shapes, the SX counters over situations whose journeys
touch the area, the 13 category groups per area, the previous evening per area — and per-area
labels and eligibility. Variants (`analysis/gen_roll_area.py`):

| variant | features | label | rows | answers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **B** | production lists | area-wide | area-wide eligible | today's forecast, re-scored against each area's label — the honest baseline |
| **L** | production lists | the area's own | the area's eligible days | is the gain in the *label*? |
| **S** | production + the area's own state | own | own | the corridor model (§14b's, on the production recipe) |
| **SC** | S + the area's category counters (AM issues) | own | own | does the per-area category block add anything? |
| **P** | S columns + area dummies, pooled over the three directions | own | 3 × own | one model for all areas — three times the rows |

Every comparison is on the **same days** (the intersection of the area's eligible OOS days), with a
paired bootstrap over days for ΔAUC, log-loss, the green / yellow / red bad-rates at the production
cuts (0.10 / 0.30) and the page's own rates (Presisjon, Dekning, pushes per day, bad rushes warned)
replayed per area with `docs/togvarsel/analysis/rushsim.py`. The area-specific block carries a
**placebo arm** (the `_c` columns dealt to the wrong days within the area, 20 permutations, local
sklearn replay of the recipe), per §14k's rule that every "adding X helps" claim carries one.

Five readings.

1. **Today's forecast loses 0.04–0.14 AUC when it is judged by a single direction.** On its own
   label it scores 0.77 at 06:30; against the west's label 0.73, the northeast's 0.70, the south's
   0.63. That gap is §2's label disagreement showing up as skill, and it is the honest number for
   what a northeast or south commuter is being told today.
2. **Training on the direction's own label does not recover it — it makes things worse.** A model
   fitted on 200–300 days of a noisier label (L) is no better than B in the west and central and
   significantly worse in the south (−0.08 at 06:30, −0.10 at 12:00, intervals excluding zero).
   Adding the direction's own ET/SX state (S, SC) does not help: the area-wide state already carries
   the system-wide morning, and per-direction state on ~300 rows buys variance. §5's placebo says
   the same in the one comparison where it can: only one cell clears it.
3. **The one real gain is the northeast at 07:00**: 0.657 → 0.734–0.739 (+0.08, CI [+0.01, +0.15]),
   consistently across S, SC and P. It decomposes: **L already takes +0.06 of it** — training on the
   northeast's own rest-of-rush label, which agrees with the network's on only 84 % of days (§2), is
   most of the gain — and the area's own 06:30–06:50 state adds ~+0.015 on top, which is what §5's
   placebo cell (+0.034, z 2.8, on the local replay) is measuring. Today's 07:00 forecast is worst
   in the northeast precisely because the network's 07–09 label is the poorest proxy there; the
   06:30 cell, on the whole-rush label, shows no such gain.
4. **Pooling is the one shape that does not lose.** P — one model per issue over all three
   directions with area dummies and the area's own state — sits within noise of B in every cell but
   one (−0.06 to +0.01, intervals including zero; 14:00 south −0.07 [−0.15, −0.01]), takes the
   northeast 07:00 gain (+0.08), and has
   the lower log-loss in 10 of 12 cells (06:30 pooled 0.469 against B's 0.517; south 0.516 against
   0.618): its probabilities are calibrated to the direction's label, which is what the green /
   yellow / red cuts read. Three times the rows is what lets it learn the direction offsets without
   losing the shared signal that the separate models (L, S) throw away. One bin does not improve
   uniformly: P's **green** is cleaner in the west (10 → 5 % bad) and the south (12 → 6 %) but not in
   the northeast at 06:30 (B 91 days → 14 %, P 46 → 17 %) — a green promise the product cannot keep
   there under the fixed 0.10 cut, so the level cuts need checking per area before they are trusted.
5. **The south is barely forecastable at the rush-hour grain.** 0.63 with the area-wide model,
   0.51–0.59 with its own; at chance in the afternoon whichever model. Its morning label correlates
   0.28 with the northeast's and 0.47 with the west's — the Østfold side has its own mornings, and
   they are not visible at 06:30 in either its own or the network's early state. A south forecast
   would be mostly yellow, and mostly wrong.

**Third wave — boosted trees on the pooled rows, stopped on cost after 3–4 of 12 folds.** The
study's tree verdict (§14c) was made on ~300 rows; P has three times that, so one honest re-check
was owed. `BOOSTED_TREE_CLASSIFIER` with the study's tuned configuration (depth 3, 200 iterations,
learning rate 0.05, subsample 0.8, min child weight 10) on P's rows and columns. **It was stopped
after 17 models**: each `CREATE MODEL` billed 22–49 GB (iterations × data, growing with the training
set), and on this on-demand project BigQuery ML's advanced-model rate ($312.50 / TB) prices that at
~$7–15 per model — 48 models would have been ~$330. What the finished folds (Oct 2025 → Jan 2026,
55–83 OOS days per direction, all winter) show is noise: 06:30 west +0.07 [−0.05, +0.20], northeast
+0.05 [−0.05, +0.16], south −0.04; 07:00 west +0.18 [+0.01, +0.36], northeast +0.14 [−0.03, +0.31],
south −0.05; the PM issues −0.37 and −0.24 in the south with intervals excluding zero. Trees on
three times the rows are not obviously worse than in the study and are not demonstrably better; a
proper answer needs the other eight folds, at a price this note does not think the question is
worth (`results/eval_area.txt` §4f). **EP**, the mean of B's and P's probabilities, is P without
the northeast gain (07:00 pooled 0.701 against P's 0.712) and is not worth its second model.

## 7. Recommendation

1. **Splitting the forecast into the three directions is a relevance change, not an accuracy
   change, and it is worth making for the northeast and the west.** Today's forecast tells a
   northeast commuter the wrong class on 12 % of mornings and a west commuter on 9 % (§2), and judged
   by their own rush it scores 0.70 / 0.73 rather than the 0.77 the page reports (§4b). A per-area
   forecast fixes the *label* — the traveller is graded on their own trains — and the pooled model
   keeps the skill: the same AUC, probabilities better calibrated to the direction's label on
   log-loss (0.469 vs 0.517), and +0.08 in the northeast at 07:00 — most of it from the label, a
   little from the direction's own state. Before the fixed cuts are trusted per area, **re-check the
   green bin per area** (northeast 06:30 reads 17 % bad under P's green) or recalibrate per area.
2. **Train ONE model per issue with area dummies and the area's own state (P), not one per area.**
   Separate per-area models lose 0.02–0.13 AUC to variance on ~300 rows and collapse in the south;
   pooling three times the rows is what preserves the shared signal. Keep central OUT of the pooled
   set (P4 costs the directions ~0.01); its stations belong to every direction, so a central
   traveller should read the network-wide forecast, which is what today's model is. Production
   `l2_reg` values stand; the per-area category block adds nothing (§5) and is not worth a serving
   twin. In the training chain this is one more spec per issue in `10_train_models.sql` reading a
   day × area table shaped like `sql/build_area_training.sql`, plus per-area eligibility (half the
   area's median call count — the July closure weeks must not become labels) and a serving twin that
   computes the `_c` columns per corridor from `togvarsel_features_today.sql`.
3. **Say the south is a weak forecast, or do not issue one.** 0.63 at best on its own label,
   0.51–0.59 with its own model, chance in the afternoon; the Østfold side's mornings are largely
   independent of the rest (corr 0.28 with the northeast) and not visible at 06:30. A south card that
   is honest about it ("liten prediksjonsevne på denne strekningen") is better than a yellow that is
   wrong four times in five; a product that only splits west / northeast and shows the south the
   network forecast is the alternative.
4. **Expect `/rushhistorikk`'s Presisjon not to move.** 20–26 % in every direction under every
   variant (§6) — it is the push-on-yellow policy, exactly as `04-presisjon` measured. What moves is
   which pushes are wrong, and the bad-rushes-warned figure in the northeast (84 % → 90 %). Judge the
   split by that and by the per-area calibration, not by Presisjon.
5. **Do not re-run**: per-area models (L, S, SC), central as an area, `l2_reg` off production,
   the realised morning in the PM issues, per-area categories, the B+P ensemble — and not boosted
   trees on the pooled rows without a budget of ~$330 and a reason to expect more than the partial
   folds showed. All measured here with intervals.
   What is not measured: attributing a central call to its train's direction (Oslo S arrivals of an
   L1 from Lillestrøm counted as northeast) — a labelling variant that would make each area's label
   include the traveller's actual Oslo S leg; a plausible next step if the split is built.

The product-side landmines for building it are in the memory note of 2026-09-13 and unchanged:
`togvarsel_issued` / `togvarsel_rounds` MERGE on `(d, issue)` only, `/rushhistorikk` is one row per
day, and the round-marker document is already nested by area.
