# A timetable cut is a bad rush: the standing-timetable baseline as label and feature (2026-09-21)

Two decisions from the product owner on 2026-09-21 set this up. The rush forecast is **not** to
be exposed on `/notification-api` yet; the job now is to get its precision and recall as good as
they can be. And **a timetable cut announced the day before counts as a bad rush.** The second
answers the open question of [02-removed-journeys](02-removed-journeys-2026-09-21.md) §5 and
turns the standing-timetable baseline into a label component and a live feature. This note
builds both and measures them, on the network models and on the pooled area model of
[01-omraader](01-omraader-2026-09-21.md).

Budget: standard-rate models only (the owner's instruction, pending tomorrow's billing report
on the boosted trees). Everything here is logistic regression; the section on cost has the tally.

## The baseline

A (line, train number) is **standing** on day d if the GTFS plan carried it on at least three of
the four previous same-weekday dates. A standing train absent from d's plan is a **cut**; a train
in d's plan that is not standing is **new**. Per window the cut is counted in planned Oslo-area
calls (taken from the train's most recent appearance) and **netted against the new trains'
calls**, so a retimed evening plan or a December timetable change reads as zero and a reduction
reads as positive. A cut is **persisting** when the same train was already missing on the
previous weekday (Monday looks back to Friday), and **first-day** otherwise.

Two label sets follow, on top of f3 from [03-labels](03-labels-2026-09-21.md) (removed trains
counted as cancelled):

| set | rule | eligible days that turn bad (of 358) |
|---|---|---|
| **f4a** | f3 + every cut | AM +31, PM +28 |
| **f4b** | f3 + first-day cuts only | AM +7, PM +9 |

f4a is dominated by planned engineering periods (late June to early July 2025 and 2026, the
Easter weeks, October 2025 on L1): 41 eligible weekdays carry a cut of 30 calls or more and 30 of
them are persisting. f4b is the "announced the day before" reading: 11 eligible weekdays, among
them 2026-03-30 (a 617-train Monday), 2026-03-06 (L2), 2025-06-23 and 2025-10-02. Which reading
the owner meant is worth a sentence back: the answer was to the day-before case, so **f4b is the
label this note recommends**, and f4a is reported because a commuter in an engineering week
is also not getting their train.

The feature is the same count, known at issue time: the GTFS partition for day d is written at
about 02:06 on d (checked for 2026-09-19 to 2026-09-24), so at 06:30 the plan for the day and
the four reference weeks are all there. A serving-time prototype (`cut_live.sql`, scratchpad)
dry-runs at **0.37 GB**. Run today at 17:00 it reads: 133 standing trains missing, 131 of them
first-day, across L1, L2, R13, R14, RE10, RE11 and others; 154 AM-rush and 223 PM-rush calls
net of additions; northeast 65/84, west 80/112, central 21/40, south 1/1. Today's morning
outcome, provisional 0.037 green, becomes about 0.16 yellow under f4b against a red 06:30
forecast.

Everything is in dev: `exp_rush_cut_trips` (one row per missing standing train and day),
`exp_rush_cut` (per day), `exp_rush_labels_fix` (all label sets), `exp_rush_area_labels_fix`
(per corridor), and the columns `y_*_f4a`, `y_*_f4b`, `cut_*`, `cut_*_new` on
`exp_rush_area_training_fix`.

## Network models (rolling origin, 12 monthly folds, 241 scored days)

Variants, all the production recipe: `base` (old labels), `BC` (old labels + the cut feature),
`F4a`/`F4b` (trained on the cut labels), `F4aC`/`F4bC` (cut labels + the cut feature). Every
model is scored on every label set; the full grid is in `analysis/results/eval_labels.txt`.

**On the recommended label, f4b:**

| issue | base | BC | F4b | F4bC | F4bC − base [95 % CI] |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 06:30 | 0.745 | 0.755 | 0.742 | **0.787** | +0.042 [−0.003, +0.095] |
| 07:00 | 0.724 | 0.730 | 0.729 | **0.772** | **+0.048 [+0.004, +0.104]** |
| 12:00 | 0.578 | 0.573 | 0.531 | **0.653** | **+0.074 [+0.009, +0.143]** |
| 14:00 | 0.594 | 0.598 | 0.565 | **0.668** | +0.074 [−0.003, +0.156] |

Push rule (p ≥ 0.10, yellow or red), f4b labels, F4bC against base: 06:30 precision 0.29 vs
0.30 with recall 0.88 vs 0.81; 07:00 0.28/0.97 vs 0.29/0.92; 12:00 0.24/0.92 vs 0.24/0.88;
14:00 0.23/0.94 vs 0.22/0.88. Recall rises five to six points at unchanged precision; the
precision ceiling is the policy one from 04-presisjon, not a model limit.

**On the old labels** F4bC is indistinguishable from base (0.769 / 0.767 / 0.595 / 0.637 against
0.773 / 0.768 / 0.573 / 0.635), so adopting it costs nothing on the scoreboard as it stands
today. **On f4a** the cut feature is worth +0.10 to +0.15 everywhere, all significant, which is
the mechanical result of a closure week being known the evening before; report it, do not sell
it.

Three things the grid says that matter for the build.

- **The feature carries the gain, not the relabelling.** F4b without the feature is at or below
  base on every issue; BC (old labels, cut feature) is within noise of base. The cut has to be in
  both the label and the feature list.
- **Log-loss moves with it**: 0.435 against 0.472 at 06:30, 0.474 against 0.529 at 12:00 on f4b.
  The level bins therefore need re-checking before the cuts are trusted, as in 01-omraader §7.
- **The PM gain is the one the product needs most.** 12:00 and 14:00 were the weak issues
  (0.57–0.64), and +0.07 there is the largest movement any approach in this folder has produced.

## Pooled area model

The same idea one level down: the cut is per line, lines map onto the directions, so the pooled
model P of 01-omraader (three directions, own state, area dummies) gets the **area's own cut**
(`cut_*_c`, `cut_*_new_c`) and the area's corrected labels. Variants: `Pf3`, `Pf4b` (corrected
area labels), `Pf4bC` (+ the area cut), `PC` (old area label + the area cut); `base` is today's
network forecast scored on each direction's label, as in 01-omraader §4a. Full grid in
`analysis/results/eval_cuts_area.txt`.

Pooled over the three directions on the f4b area label, paired ΔAUC:

| issue | Pf4bC − base [95 % CI] | Pf4bC − P [95 % CI] |
|---|---|---|
| 06:30 | +0.008 [−0.026, +0.042] | +0.004 [−0.020, +0.031] |
| 07:00 | **+0.064 [+0.033, +0.096]** | **+0.025 [+0.001, +0.050]** |
| 12:00 | +0.010 [−0.032, +0.053] | **+0.053 [+0.017, +0.093]** |
| 14:00 | +0.010 [−0.036, +0.058] | **+0.050 [+0.017, +0.086]** |

Per direction the feature does what the cut counts say it should. **West**, where the cuts land
(mean first-day PM cut 3.3 calls a day against 1.0–1.6 elsewhere), moves a lot: 07:00 0.735 →
0.810, 12:00 0.592 → 0.630, 14:00 0.620 → 0.667 against base. **Northeast** is unchanged
(0.681 / 0.759 / 0.555 / 0.597, the same to three decimals with or without the cut) and
**south** stays the weak direction (0.626 / 0.653 / 0.550 / 0.526). The push-rule precision is
0.19–0.28 everywhere, recall 0.86–0.96, and no variant separates from the others by more than
two points on either.

Read against the network table above, the area model with the cut **does not beat the network
model with the cut**: on the same label F4bC gains +0.04 to +0.07 over base at every issue,
Pf4bC gains that only at 07:00. So the ranking of 01-omraader holds after the cut is added: the
split is a relevance change, the pooled shape is the one to build if it is built, and the cut
belongs in the network chain first.

## What was built (same evening, working tree, uncommitted)

The cut is in the production chain and the serving query, in dev, with the models trained as
**candidates only** (`togvarsel_m_cand_*`, not promoted; see the last paragraph for why).

- **`data/queries/togvarsel/03b_plan.sql`** (new) → `disruptions.togvarsel_plan_cut`: per day
  the removed trains (`*_miss`, GTFS trains absent from ET on a line that ran) and the cut
  (`*_cut`, `*_cut_new`) exactly as defined above, from 2025-04-10. Sorts between 03 and 04 in
  `retrain.sh`'s glob; ~21 GB of GTFS per rebuild, about $0.15.
- **`03_day_windows.sql`**: `observed` (a time that is not `estimated`) and `silent` (an
  estimated call whose journey went quiet > 10 min before it was due) beside `has_actual`, and
  the label numerator / denominator `n_t10_lab` / `n_res_lab` under the corrected rule. Every
  feature column is untouched.
- **`04_days.sql`**: `am_t10` / `pm_t10` are now the f4b labels (`n_t10_lab + miss + cut_new`
  over `n_res_lab + miss + cut_new`, rounded to 4 as before); the old values stay as
  `am_t10_raw` / `pm_t10_raw`; the six `cut_*` columns ride along. `08_features_cat.sql` does the
  same for the 07:00 label (`am_t10_rest0700`, raw as before, old value as `_raw`).
- **`07_features.sql`** carries `cut_*` from the day table; **`10_train_models.sql`** has them in
  all four lists, linear, with the reason in its header.
- **`disruptions/api/togvarsel_features_today.sql`** computes the six live from GTFS (today, the
  previous weekday and four reference weeks each); the table requires a partition filter spelled
  out as literals, and BigQuery refuses the correlated `EXISTS` inside this script, so the
  persistence test is a join. Run once in dev at 22:02: 154 / 154 / 117 / 117 / 223 / 223.
- **`disruptions/api/togvarsel.py`**: one new reason in both rule sets, «færre rushtidsavganger
  enn vanlig i dagens ruteplan», when the first-day cut in the issue's window is 20 calls or more
  (about two trains). Positive in every model, so it passes the 2026-09-03 rule. Suite green.

Rebuilt in dev: the chain reproduces the experiment's f4b labels on 358 eligible days except
two (2025-04-14, where the chain's standing set reaches four weeks further back and sees the
Easter closure's first day; 2025-05-06, an 11-call netting difference). Bad days now 76 / 83 / 75
against 64 / 73 / 59 under the old rule.

**The candidate models** (`10_train_models.sql`, 18 min, holdout = the last 56 days, f4b
labels): chosen `l2_reg` 30 / 3 / 10 / 10, holdout AUC **0.861 / 0.860 / 0.728 / 0.715** and
log-loss 0.424 / 0.399 / 0.511 / 0.489 for 06:30 / 07:00 / 12:00 / 14:00. The launch table in
03-implementasjon read 0.81 / 0.81 / 0.75 / 0.75 on a summer holdout of the old labels, so the
two are not comparable; the rolling table above is the evidence. The cut weights are positive in
all four (first-day cut 0.007 to 0.013 per call, the persisting remainder near zero), which is
what the reason rule requires. Smoke test on today's live feature row at 22:20: the 06:30
candidate says 0.80 where the live model says 0.49, the 14:00 candidate 0.47 where the live one
says 0.12; today's morning is yellow under f4b and its afternoon will read the 223 cut calls.

**Why the models are not promoted.** `ML.PREDICT` fails the whole scoring job on a model whose
contract names a column its input lacks, and the deployed dev app rebuilds
`togvarsel_features_today` from the SQL in its image, which does not carry `cut_*` until this
change is deployed. Order: deploy the serving query, then run `retrain.sh` (the promotion loop
is what this run skipped). `retrain.sh`'s header says so.

**The outcome passes followed in a second PR** (`feat/rushhistorikk-cut-outcomes`, after #213
merged): both `togvarsel_outcomes` queries carry the f4b rule — the settled one with all three
terms, the provisional one without the silent-tail term (the planned table has no snapshot time;
named as its fifth skew) — with the plan terms computed inline from GTFS for the span (~2.4 GB per
14-day chunk, cap raised to 6 GiB). A settled row is never rewritten, so
`data/queries/togvarsel/regrade_outcomes.py` re-opens a date range and re-settles it through the
module's own path; dev was re-graded from 2026-08-29. Until prd is deployed and re-graded, its
page grades the new models against labels they were not trained on.
